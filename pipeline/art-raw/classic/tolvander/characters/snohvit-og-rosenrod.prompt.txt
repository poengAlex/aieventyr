Character: the Maiden. A slender young woman of about twenty with snow-pale skin, rosy-red cheeks and lips, a soft oval face, a small straight nose and large dark-grey eyes with long black lashes. Her raven-black hair hangs in one thick, loose braid over her shoulder, tied with a strip of white linen. She wears a plain dark-teal homespun wool dress with a deep pocket, where the handle of a small silver spoon peeks out, a long grey wool shawl and thin, worn brown leather shoes.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. The royal manors are clusters of dark log buildings with turf roofs, carved gables and small leaded windows around a yard. The Queen's sleigh is small and red, drawn by two brown horses. The thirteen silver spoons are small and exactly alike. As wild ducks, the twelve brothers are mallard drakes with glossy bottle-green heads, white neck rings, chestnut breasts and grey backs. As men they look alike: pale, with tousled raven-black hair, bottle-green caps and chestnut-brown tunics; the eldest is the tallest and has a short black beard. Bog cotton grows in fluffy white tufts, and the caps, shirts and kerchiefs sewn from it are pure white. The snake pit is an old, dank walled stone yard behind the young King's manor. The three children are two small boys with fair curls and a baby girl with black hair.

This story's season, light and colours: One long tale through many seasons: a hushed white winter day of fresh snow under a low, pale sun at the start; mossy spruce-green forest and warm firelight in the brothers' cabin; the bog at sunrise, a sea of white bog cotton in thin mist and pale-gold light; cool grey stone and candlelight at the young King's manor; a smoky orange pyre glow under a slate-blue dusk; and a clear, fresh spring morning at the end. Accents of pure white, blood red and raven black.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
