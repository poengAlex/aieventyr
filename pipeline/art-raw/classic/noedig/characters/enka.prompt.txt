Character: the Widow. A tall, big, well-fed farm wife of about sixty with a large round face, a double chin, pink cheeks, a big mouth always open to talk, bright little inquisitive eyes and iron-grey ringlets under a large round black bonnet trimmed with lace and tied under her chins. She wears a wine-red wool dress with silver buttons and a green silk shawl, and she always holds a long brass ear trumpet to one ear.

Story world: Old rural Norway at sowing time, in the Ringerike country. The Farmer and his Wife have a small, poor farm with one brown field. A dusty road leads to the market town, where the Butcher has his shop and yard. Far along the roads stand a brand-new log house with no windows that still smells of resin, a small plain cottage, and a big farm with a storehouse and a field being ploughed. The Wife's hen is a scrawny, half-bald speckled hen. The widow's blue horse is a sturdy blue-grey dappled horse with a black mane and tail, harnessed to a small two-wheeled wooden cart; Per the Third's horse is chestnut-brown. The sieve is a big, round, shallow sieve with a wooden rim.

This story's season, light and colours: Late April at sowing time: a dry, breezy spring with pale sunshine and drifting cloud, bare brown fields and fresh dark furrows, birches in their first mouse-ear green, the last grey snow patches in the shade of the spruce, yellow coltsfoot in the ditches and dusty roads. At the end, a heavy grey sky that promises rain. Accents: the Wife's rust-orange skirt, white feathers and the blue-grey horse.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
