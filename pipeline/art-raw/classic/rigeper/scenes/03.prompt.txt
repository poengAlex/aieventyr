Picture: In the low-beamed kitchen of an old mill, lit by the fire and one small window, an old miller with a flour-dusted grey beard and a white cap and his small, thin old wife with a grey braid wound round her head bend over the open chest on the table. Inside it, on a wool blanket, lies a beautiful baby boy with a wisp of strawberry-blond hair, waving his little fists. The old couple stare in wonder, the wife's hands pressed to her cheeks. Through the window, the big wooden waterwheel stands still.

Story world: Old rural Norway, over many years. Rich Per the pedlar's big farm lies above a river; just below it stands a small grey log mill, and many miles downstream a second, older mill with a big wooden waterwheel. The chest is a small, finely made box of polished dark wood with brass corners, its seams sealed with black pitch and an iron key in the lock. Per's letters are folded papers sealed with red wax. The robbers' farm is a shabby log farmhouse deep in a dark spruce forest; the young couple's farm lies up under a bare ridge. The royal manors are clusters of dark log halls with carved gables. Far away runs a great, wide river with no boat or bridge, and on the steep bank above it stands a big black stone castle. The three feathers are long, shining golden feathers.

This story's season, light and colours: Early winter in the river valleys: black, fast, ice-cold rivers with low mist hanging over them, the first thin snow on the banks and fields, frosted reeds and bare birches and alders under iron-grey skies, and short, pale afternoons fading into long blue dusks. Indoors: amber lamplight and firelight. In the black castle: slate-dark gloom, ember-red firelight and the glint of heaped gold.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
