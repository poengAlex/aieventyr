Character: the Garden Boy. A slim young man of about nineteen with milk-pale skin, bright red cheeks, an oval face, a neat, narrow nose, big dark-brown eyes and thick level black eyebrows, whose hair is completely hidden under a huge, shaggy wig of pale grey-green beard lichen from old spruces that hangs in long, wispy, tangled strands over his ears, neck and forehead, so he looks wretched and scruffy. He wears a faded cornflower-blue sleeveless jerkin, a torn oatmeal smock, a small red neckerchief, earth-stained charcoal trousers and scuffed brown ankle boots.

Story world: Old rural Norway. The widow's son walks from a poor valley into endless spruce forest, where the Stranger's house stands alone: tall and gloomy, of smoke-blackened logs, with four forbidden rooms off its long hallway. On a shelf above the door of the first lies a whip of thorny wild-rose briar; in the second, a round grey stone as big as a loaf and a small brown clay water jug with a cork; in the third, a trapdoor over a huge round copper cauldron that boils with no fire under it; in the fourth, the black horse's plank stall. The ointment is kept in a small round blue clay jar. Near the royal manor, a great hollow linden stands alone in a green forest glade, and below the manor lies a wide brown bog. The royal handkerchief is fine white linen with a small gold crown embroidered in one corner.

This story's season, light and colours: High summer: long, bright days, green meadows and wild roses in bloom, and blue-green shade under the spruces. Inside the Stranger's house: dim candlelight, smoke-dark timber and the warm glow of copper. The bog is rust-red and olive under tall white clouds, and the battle days end in dusty golden evening light. Accents: wild-rose pink, rust orange and gleaming copper.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
