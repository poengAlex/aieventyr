Character: the Husband. A huge, slow, kind-hearted farmer of about fifty-five, tall and broad as a barn door, with a long, calm face, a big round nose, sleepy pale-blue eyes under bushy eyebrows and a big, fan-shaped, straw-coloured beard, his straw-coloured hair combed straight back. He wears a black-and-white speckled knitted jumper with silver clasps at the neck, brown knee breeches, white knitted stockings and heavy boots, and has big, work-worn hands.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, at threshing and ploughing time. A small farm on a green hillside: a dark log farmhouse with a turf roof, a covered porch and small-paned windows; across the yard, a smaller, crooked log cottage, the Other House, and a big grey barn with a wide double door and a threshing floor. Below them a meadow slopes down past a fence of slanting grey poles to a brown field with a wooden plough. The farmhouse main room has a long scrubbed table with benches, an open hearth and a plank door to a small side room. Sour-cream porridge is thick and creamy-white, served in a big round wooden dish with a golden pool of melted butter in the middle. Lefse is a big, round, thin, soft flatbread.

This story's season, light and colours: Early autumn at threshing and ploughing time: golden stubble and a green-gold meadow, birches turning yellow, red rowan berries, fresh-turned chocolate-brown earth and a clear blue sky with warm, low afternoon sun. Indoors: warm hearth light, scrubbed pale wood and the creamy white of the porridge.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
