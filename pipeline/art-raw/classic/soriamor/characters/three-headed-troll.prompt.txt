Character: the Three-Headed Troll. A big, pear-shaped troll about twice as tall as a man, with knobbly blue-grey skin like wet slate, long dangling arms and huge flat feet. He has exactly three heads side by side on his broad shoulders, each with a big round nose: the left head is sleepy, with droopy eyelids and a floppy faded-red nightcap; the middle head is grumpy, with one bushy black eyebrow; the right head is surprised, with round eyes and a tuft of white hair. He wears a patched brown leather jerkin tied with rope.

Story world: Old rural Norway and a strange, empty land far across the sea. Halvor's home is a tiny, poor turf-roofed log cottage on a hillside above the fjord, below a big farm and a lake. Across the sea lies a land of fields and meadows without a soul, where three troll castles stand far apart, each grander than the last, with light in every window. In each castle a huge old troll sword with a plain iron hilt hangs on the wall, with a small green glass flask on a leather cord beside it. The wishing ring is a heavy silver ring with one round blue stone; the ring left in its place is a slim gold band. Halvor's horse is a shaggy, bony dun horse. The magic boots are cracked old brown leather boots. Soria Moria is a castle of glittering golden towers far beyond forests and mountains.

This story's season, light and colours: Early autumn: long blue dusks and clear moonlit nights, with indigo skies, silver moonlight, violet shadows and warm golden light glowing from castle windows far away. Daylight is cool and silvery; indoors, amber firelight gleams on gold and silver. Accents: the youngest princess's red and rose-and-ember sunsets over the fjord.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening.
