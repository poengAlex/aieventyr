Character: the Fox. A slim, light-footed, long-legged red fox with sleek, bright flame-red winter fur, a snow-white chin, throat and chest, black lower legs and paws, tall pointed ears with black backs, narrow amber eyes with a sly sideways glance and a long, pointed snout with long whiskers. His big, bushy red tail ends in a clean white tip.

Story world: A snowy spruce forest in old Norway, long ago, where the animals can talk. Below the forest lies the Frozen Lake, a small forest lake under thick, snow-dusted ice. A little way up the hill is a sunny slope, an open clearing where the low winter sun shines, with one dense, dark spruce bush at its upper edge. The fox's stolen fish are five fat silver fish tied together by their tails with a piece of string. The butter tub is a small, round wooden tub with a flat lid and two bent-wood hoops, about as tall as the fox and full of yellow butter. The bear and the fox are natural wild animals without clothes: they walk on four legs, but can sit up and use their front paws like hands.

This story's season, light and colours: Deep midwinter in a spruce forest: a bitter, frosty morning of hoarfrost and pale blue light over the frozen lake, then, just before Christmas, a still, clear day when the low sun lies warm and golden on a snowy slope, with long blue shadows towards evening. Accents: the fox's flame red, butter yellow and dark spruce green.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.

Rules:
- No text of any kind: no letters, words, numbers, signatures, captions, speech bubbles or logos.
- Every named character must match their model sheet and description exactly: the same face, hair, colours, clothes and proportions.
- Suitable for readers of all ages: no blood, wounds or gore, and no killing shown. Show danger and cruelty through atmosphere, posture and suspense, never through injury.
