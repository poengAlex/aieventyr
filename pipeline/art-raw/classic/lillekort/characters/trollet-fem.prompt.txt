Character: the Five-Headed Troll. A huge, bloated sea troll about five times as tall as a man, with dusky purple-grey skin like an old mussel shell, crusted with barnacles and hung with strands of dark seaweed, a great sagging belly, long dangling arms and flat, webbed feet. It has exactly five heads on five thick, wrinkled necks, each with a long drooping nose, small round yellow eyes and a tuft of shaggy grey hair. It wears a kilt of old brown fishing net and carries a thick iron bar.

Story world: Old rural Norway on the open coast, long ago. Lillekort and his twin are born in a wretched, crooked cottage with a sagging turf roof at the edge of a birch forest. Far away, the King's manor stands on a slope above a wide sandy shore by the open sea: dark log buildings with turf roofs around a long hall, all hung with black cloth while the princess is promised to three sea trolls, who come over the sea in black ships with black sails. Lillekort's sword has a golden hilt and a plain brown leather sheath. His magic ship is a wooden ship with two masts and white sails; when small, it fits in his pocket. The princess's three secret garments are of gold, silver and brass. The iron cable is a colossal chain. At the bottom of the sea stands a rock mountain with a door.

This story's season, light and colours: Late autumn on the open coast: blustery days with yellow birch leaves and scarlet rowan berries, and a dark-green sea with white-capped waves. The three Thursday evenings lie under a huge, low orange moon, with silver sea mist drifting over the sand. At the wedding, candlelight makes gold, silver and brass glitter. Under the sea: cold green-blue gloom and the red glow of the troll's oven.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.

Rules:

- Suitable for readers of all ages: no blood, wounds or gore, and no killing shown. Show danger and cruelty through atmosphere, posture and suspense, never through injury.
