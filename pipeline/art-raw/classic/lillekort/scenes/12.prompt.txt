Create one new illustration for an illustrated folk-tale book, showing the moment described below.

Picture: A very wide view of the Shore at night. The Fifteen-Headed Troll comes wading out of the sea, much, much bigger than the others, so enormous that it blots out the sky and the orange moon, all fifteen heads in their pyramid glaring down, waves breaking around its stump-like legs. Tiny Lillekort stands alone on the sand far below with his feet apart. The Younger Princess clings to her flat stone, and in the bending oak Sir Red hugs a branch with his eyes squeezed shut.

Characters in this picture (nobody else, unless the picture description mentions them):
- the Fifteen-Headed Troll: The biggest sea troll of all, as tall as a hill, with dark peat-brown, lumpy skin like a bog, shaggy grey hair on its shoulders, a round belly and legs like old tree stumps. It has exactly fifteen heads stacked in a pyramid, five on its shoulders, then four, three, two and one on top, each with a round red nose, small blinking amber eyes and a shock of grey hair. It wears a huge patched sailcloth tunic tied with a ship's rope and carries a gigantic iron bar.
- Lillekort: A tiny, bold boy only days old but no bigger than a child of four, with the sturdy, natural proportions of a small child, a round face, rosy cheeks, a small button nose, big bright cornflower-blue eyes, a determined little chin and a mop of golden curls. An oversized knitted cap with blue-and-white stripes flops over one ear. He wears his big brothers' old rags: a far-too-big, much-patched grey-brown linen shirt with the sleeves rolled up, baggy patched brown breeches tied with string and big, worn-out leather shoes.
- the Younger Princess: A slim, lively princess of about eighteen with a round face, a pointed little chin, a small turned-up nose, big grey eyes, pale skin with pink cheeks and short, bouncy platinum-blonde curls. On her curls sits a small gold circlet shaped like a ring of oak leaves. She wears an emerald-green wool gown with long, narrow sleeves and a high round collar embroidered with tiny gold leaves, a thin gold belt and a string of small white pearls.
- Sir Red: A big, burly, barrel-chested knight of about forty, a head taller than most men, with a broad, puffy face that easily turns bright red, a big bulbous nose, small shifty pale-blue eyes under bushy brows, curly red hair and a huge, bushy fiery-red beard. He wears polished steel armour over a red tunic, a long bright-red cloak and heavy boots, and always has a big white handkerchief tucked into his belt.

Setting:
- the Shore: A wide, pale sandy beach below the royal manor, curving along the open sea. Right at the water's edge lies one big, flat, grey boulder, and at the top of the beach stands a huge, gnarled old oak with wide, spreading branches and yellow-brown autumn leaves. Tufts of beach grass grow in the low dunes, and a grassy slope rises behind to the dark log buildings of the manor.

Story world: Old rural Norway on the open coast, long ago. Lillekort and his twin are born in a wretched, crooked cottage with a sagging turf roof at the edge of a birch forest. Far away, the King's manor stands on a slope above a wide sandy shore by the open sea: dark log buildings with turf roofs around a long hall, all hung with black cloth while the princess is promised to three sea trolls, who come over the sea in black ships with black sails. Lillekort's sword has a golden hilt and a plain brown leather sheath. His magic ship is a wooden ship with two masts and white sails; when small, it fits in his pocket. The princess's three secret garments are of gold, silver and brass. The iron cable is a colossal chain. At the bottom of the sea stands a rock mountain with a door.

This story's season, light and colours: Late autumn on the open coast: blustery days with yellow birch leaves and scarlet rowan berries, and a dark-green sea with white-capped waves. The three Thursday evenings lie under a huge, low orange moon, with silver sea mist drifting over the sand. At the wedding, candlelight makes gold, silver and brass glitter. Under the sea: cold green-blue gloom and the red glow of the troll's oven.

The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening.
