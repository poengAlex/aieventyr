Character: the Eldest Brother. A tall, gawky young man of about twenty-two with a long, bony face, a big chin, heavy-lidded eyes, a long nose, a lazy, lopsided grin and thick, wavy dark-blond hair that sticks up at the back. He wears a red-and-white checked homespun shirt with rolled sleeves, baggy brown breeches held up by a single strap and a wide, frayed straw hat, and he goes barefoot.

Story world: Old rural Norway at midsummer. The poor family lives in a tumbledown turf-roofed hut in a spruce forest. The royal farm lies in a green valley: log buildings stained ox-blood red, with turf roofs, carved gables, broad front steps and a long stable. The seven colts are glossy chestnut colts with flaxen manes and tails; the youngest and smallest has a white star on his forehead. As princes they are seven brothers from about twelve to twenty-two, with their sister's strawberry-blonde hair and shining white-and-gold clothes. The hag sits in a shallow cleft at the foot of a grey cliff beside a forest path. A huge white birch stump in a clearing hides the door to a small earthen room with a hearth, a great rusty sword and a little clay jar. Beyond a long wooden bridge over a river stands a small, dark, tarred church with a tall tower.

This story's season, light and colours: Midsummer: long, pale-gold days, a low sun that hardly sets and bright, pale nights under a lilac sky; violet mountains, purple heather, birch woods and dewy grass, with long golden shadows in the evenings. The wedding glows with warm candlelight.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
