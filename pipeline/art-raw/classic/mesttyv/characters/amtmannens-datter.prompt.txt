Character: the Bailiff's Daughter. A kind, clever young woman of about twenty with a heart-shaped face, a dimple in one cheek, lively grey-green eyes that always seem to be laughing, a small straight nose and nut-brown hair in two braids coiled into round buns over her ears. She wears a long apricot-coloured wool dress with puffed sleeves and a white collar embroidered with little sprigs of lily of the valley.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. The cottager's tiny, crooked turf-roofed hut stands alone not far from where the road splits in three. Deep in a great spruce forest lies the robbers' big, dark timbered house with a blazing stone fireplace. The farmer's three oxen are big, reddish-brown and white-faced, with short curved horns, and walk the Forest Road on a rope. The Bailiff lives at the Manor Farm: a white manor house, a kitchen with an open hearth and a spit, a long red stable of twelve stalls for his twelve dun fjord horses and a dark goose house full of white geese; he rides a big dapple-grey horse. The parson's vicarage has a great old maple in the garden. The barrel is a big oak barrel on a rickety two-wheeled cart, pulled by a bony old brown mare in a harness of willow withies and broom twigs.

This story's season, light and colours: Late autumn: sleet-grey skies over wet, dark spruce forest, bare birches with a few last yellow leaves, frost-rimed mornings and muddy roads, and long, cold nights in deep blue moonlight lit by warm amber lanterns, candles and firelight. Accents: the thief's bottle-green, the Bailiff's canary yellow and the white of geese and bedsheets.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening.
