Character: the Smith. A stocky old blacksmith of about sixty-five, short and broad, with huge, sooty forearms, a wide, soot-smudged face, crinkly brown eyes under thick grey eyebrows and a long, bushy charcoal-grey beard singed at the tips. He has a fuzz of short grey hair, and small round wire spectacles sit pushed up on his forehead. He wears a heavy, scorched leather apron over a faded red homespun shirt with rolled-up sleeves, sooty black trousers and big wooden clogs.

Story world: Old rural Norway on an autumn evening. A narrow dirt road winds between hazel bushes and birches, past stubble fields and grey stone walls. The boy cracks hazelnuts as he walks; the worm-eaten nut is an ordinary brown hazelnut with one tiny round wormhole, which he plugs with a little whittled wooden peg. By the road stands the smithy: a low log building with a turf roof and a stone chimney, a wide open door, a glowing forge with leather bellows, a big black anvil on a tree stump, and a rack of hammers from tiny to huge, the biggest a great sledgehammer as long as a man's leg.

This story's season, light and colours: Late October at dusk: bare birches holding their last rust-brown leaves, hazel bushes gone yellow, a cold violet-and-rose sky with the first star, frost starting in the ruts of the road and long blue shadows. Inside the smithy: the orange glow of the forge, sparks and swirling dust against sooty dark walls.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
