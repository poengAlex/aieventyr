Character: the Prince. A broad-shouldered prince of about twenty-two with a proud, stubborn face, a strong jaw, a slightly hooked nose, bright blue eyes under straight fair brows and thick, wavy honey-blond hair swept back from his forehead and falling to his collar, under a small gold crown. He wears a heather-purple wool tunic with gold trim over a cream linen shirt, a short dark-green cloak fastened with a round gold brooch, black hose and soft black leather boots.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. The princess grows up at her father's palace, a long, dark timber hall with turf roofs, a byre and forest pastures climbing to the blue fells. The Blue Bull keeps a small, folded white cloth in his left ear; spread on the grass, it sets itself with food. Far away lie three magic woods, one of copper, one of silver and one of gold, where every trunk, leaf and flower is metal and gold drips from the leaves. Each troll wears a curved cow horn of healing ointment at its belt. At the journey's end, a sheer grey cliff looks down on a great castle of pale-grey stone with green copper roofs and a tarred stave church close by. In the castle, a steep, dark oak staircase climbs from the sooty kitchen to the Prince's chamber. The gold shoes are small, pointed and shining.

This story's season, light and colours: Cool, clear early autumn under high, pale-blue skies: blue-grey fells, birches turning yellow and purple heather, with the bull's deep, glossy blue as the strongest colour until the magic woods blaze in polished copper, frosty silver and molten gold. The castle kitchen is dim, sooty and firelit; the church Sundays are bright, windy and fresh.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
