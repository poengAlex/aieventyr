Character: the Boy. A small, skinny boy of about eleven with a narrow, pale face, cheeks reddened by the cold, big sticking-out ears, bright blue eyes, a stubborn little frown and white-blond hair that sticks up in tufts. He wears a grey knitted jumper several sizes too big, patched dark-brown trousers, thick grey socks, heavy scuffed boots and a long brown knitted scarf wound twice round his neck.

Story world: Old rural Norway at the first frost of late autumn. The boy and his mother live in a small, grey, weathered log cottage with a turf roof; beside it stands a little storehouse on posts with a steep wooden stair up to its door. Far to the north, on a bare, windswept fell where every tree leans south, stands the North Wind's house, a low, crooked log house with a sod roof weighed down with stones. On the road between them lies the inn, a big, low log house with a lantern at the door and a goat shed behind. The meal is carried in a grey sack. The magic cloth is a square white linen tablecloth with a red woven zigzag border, and the swapped one looks exactly the same. The stick is a knobbly old stick of pale birch wood, about as long as the boy is tall.

This story's season, light and colours: Blustery late autumn at the first frost: rimed grass and bare birches, cold blue-grey skies with fast, torn clouds, and snow already lying on the northern fells where the North Wind lives. Indoors, warm firelight and glowing embers against cold blue windows. Accents: flour white, lingonberry red and pewter grey.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
