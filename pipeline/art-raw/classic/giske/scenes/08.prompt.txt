Story world: Old rural Norway, in a scattered farming parish of grey log farms with turf roofs among fields, spruce forest and bogs. The widower's small farm has a low log house with a stone chimney and a field of tall, ripe hemp below it. Beyond the farms lies a great, dark spruce forest. The farms that Giske and the thieves raid have a sheep shed built wall to wall with the farmer's sleeping room, a goose house of grey logs and, at the rich farmer's at the forest edge, a stabbur: a dark timber storehouse on posts with a steep little stair, hung inside with hams and sausages above shelves of round pea bread. The turnip patch is a clearing in the woods on a slope, with old stumps and rows of purple turnips, above a soft brown bog of tussocks and stumps.

This story's season, light and colours: August, between haymaking and harvest: a hot, hazy afternoon over the green-gold hemp; then three moonlit nights in deep indigo and blue-black, with a thin sickle moon, cold starlight and the small yellow glow of a lantern or candle; and a pearl-grey Sunday dawn with low mist over the brown bog and dew on purple turnips. Accents: greasy soot black, plum purple and turnip violet.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike.
