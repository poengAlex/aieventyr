Character: Saint Peter. A tall, broad-shouldered old fisherman of about seventy with a weathered, ruddy face, a big round nose, pale blue eyes under bushy white eyebrows and a thick, curly white beard. He wears a navy-blue fisherman's cap with a short peak, a long rust-brown coat over a grey sailcloth smock, rolled-up dark trousers and sturdy boots. A big, old iron key hangs from a leather cord at his belt. No halo.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, when Our Lord and Saint Peter still walked the earth. The smithy is a squat, soot-blackened log building with a turf roof, a crooked stone chimney and wide double doors, beside a dusty country road through farmland. A gnarled old pear tree heavy with yellow pears grows against its sunny wall. Inside are a brick forge with a big leather bellows, an anvil on a stump, tools on the walls and, in the corner, a big, deep armchair of worn dark-red leather. The steel-wire purse is a small, round coin purse of woven silver-grey steel wire with a snap clasp. Far along the road, the way forks: one road climbs into white clouds to the tall white gate of Heaven; the other dips into a smoky, rocky gully to the gate of Hell, a tall, crooked, soot-black iron gate with nine big locks.

This story's season, light and colours: Blazing summer heat in a dusty farming valley: glaring white-gold afternoons, parched straw-yellow fields, an ochre dust road and ripe yellow pears among dusty leaves. Inside the smithy: soot-black and charcoal lit by ember-orange glow and showers of sparks. At the end, soft white cloud and pale gold light at the gate of Heaven, and a smoky plum-purple haze at the gate of Hell.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening. Kings and princesses wear modest medieval finery. Careful composition with depth and atmosphere, one clear focal point and room for the landscape. Not cute or cartoonish, not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
