Place: the Hill Farm. A small, poor hill farm at the edge of a birch-and-spruce wood: a low grey log cowshed with a turf roof and a small hatch high in its wall, a grey split-rail fence of slanting poles along a dewy meadow, a tall white birch whose lowest branch reaches out over the fence, and a slope of heather and bog rising behind towards a bare moor and a dark spruce ridge.

Story world: Old rural Norway, where the birds of farm, forest and moor can talk. The Rooster, the Cuckoo and the Black Grouse have bought one cow together and agreed that whoever wakes first in the morning shall have her. She grazes the meadow of a small hill farm at the edge of a birch-and-spruce wood. The Rooster lives in the hen loft of the cowshed, the Cuckoo in a tall birch at the edge of the wood, and the Black Grouse on the heather moor above the farm. The fence is a Norwegian skigard: grey poles laid slanting between pairs of upright stakes. The birds and the cow are natural animals without clothes.

This story's season, light and colours: A cold, clear May dawn: pale primrose and rose light rising over a dark spruce ridge, grey dew on the grass, the first tiny mouse-ear leaves on the birches, thin mist in the hollows and long blue shadows. Accents: the Rooster's scarlet comb, the Black Grouse's red brows and the Cow's russet coat.

Art style: Classic Norwegian folk-tale book illustration: fine pen-and-ink line work with transparent watercolour washes on warm, slightly textured off-white paper, in the tradition of 19th-century Scandinavian book illustration, like the plates of an old printed book of folk tales, with a deep red that comes back as the accent. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Naturalistic proportions and expressive, characterful faces; every character has their own face, build, age and hair exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Muted natural palette of fjord teal, slate blue, moss green, lichen grey, honey ochre, lingonberry red, ink black and birch white, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, with the warm cream paper showing through the washes, with dramatic Nordic light: low sun, long shadows, blue dusk and firelight. Old rural Norway: dark spruce and pine forests, steep mountains, fjords, turf-roofed log farms, carved wood, homespun wool and linen. Trolls are enormous, ancient and earthy, like pieces of the landscape come alive, made of moss, bark, stone and lichen, and more grotesque-comic than frightening.
