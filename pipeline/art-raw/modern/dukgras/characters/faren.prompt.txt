Character: the Chairman. A tall, thin, elderly self-made industrialist of about seventy with narrow, sloping shoulders, a long, lined face, a long pointed nose, shrewd grey eyes under thin arched eyebrows and a neat, pointed grey goatee. He wears a deep-blue three-piece suit, a silk tie patterned with rosemaling flowers in red, white and yellow and polished dark-blue shoes, and keeps a small brass folding magnifier, a thread counter, in his waistcoat pocket.

Story world: Present-day Norway in early summer. The family conglomerate began with one loom in a basement; now it owns three factories, a glass-fronted head office and a white villa by the water behind a wrought-iron gate. The rest area is a roadside café with a car park on a main road; behind it lies a meadow of long grass and wildflowers with round tussocks. Under one tussock is the Doll's home: a small room of grass with flowers growing from the ceiling and a single tiny chair. The tiny shirt is white linen, no longer than a car key. Her carriage is a polished silver spoon with a harness of sewing thread, pulled by two white mice. The old road is a narrow gravel road along a dark lake, squeezed between a rock wall and wet stones, with the new road high above.

This story's season, light and colours: Early summer with long, light days: fresh green grass and wildflowers (buttercups, clover, cow parsley) behind a grey tarmac car park and a low golden evening sun; the lake dark and cold under a drizzly grey sky; the homecoming at the villa in steady summer rain; the wedding on a pale, bright midsummer evening by the water. Accents: polished silver, sky blue and grass green.

Art style: Contemporary Scandinavian editorial illustration: flat, graphic shapes with subtle risograph grain and a little hand-drawn texture, clean confident lines and a limited palette of spot inks led by deep petrol blue, with fjord teal, lingonberry red, honey mustard, sage green, warm paper off-white and ink charcoal, like the pages of a risograph-printed book, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, in the cool, clear Nordic light of today. Present-day Norway: fjord towns, suburbs, farms with tractors, cabins, ferries, roadside petrol stations, offices and flats, grey rain and bright snow, with people in modern clothes such as rain jackets, knitted sweaters, hoodies, suits and work wear. The magic of the tale appears plainly in this everyday world: trolls, talking animals and enchantments look real and slightly absurd, drawn in the same flat style.
