Place: the Too-Ordinary Farm. A farm that looks a little too ordinary, under a flat white sky: a white two-storey farmhouse, a red barn, a bare flagpole with no flag, a gravel yard with an old stone well and a big wooden tub beside it, and no tractor, no dog, no car and nobody about. Everything is neat and silent.

Story world: Present-day rural Norway. At home: a red house on a hillside, with a garden, an old stone well without a lid and an old plank outbuilding that was once a pigsty. The stepmother sells hand-spun yarn online. At the bottom of the well lies another world: an endless summer meadow with no houses or power lines, crossed by an old-fashioned fence of woven brushwood twigs, with an apple tree and a long pole by its trunk. Far across it lies a farm that looks too ordinary. The old woman lives there with her daughter, a younger copy of her with the same hooked nose. The sieve is a round wooden frame with a mesh bottom. The three small chests are gaudy like souvenirs, red, green and blue, with heavy old locks; three tiny crosses are scratched into the lock of the blue one.

This story's season, light and colours: Two worlds. At home: a warm June afternoon on a green hillside, with low sun on the old stone well, and later the dusk-lit living room. Below the well: an endless meadow in hazy, timeless summer light, full of flowers and bumblebees; the too-ordinary farm silent under a flat white sky; a dim cowshed. At the end, an old outbuilding glowing gold through every crack. Accents: the Girl's blue bandana and dress, a mustard-yellow hoodie, and the red, green and blue chests.

Art style: Contemporary Scandinavian editorial illustration: flat, graphic shapes with subtle risograph grain and a little hand-drawn texture, clean confident lines and a limited palette of spot inks led by deep petrol blue, with fjord teal, lingonberry red, honey mustard, sage green, warm paper off-white and ink charcoal, like the pages of a risograph-printed book, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, in the cool, clear Nordic light of today. Present-day Norway: fjord towns, suburbs, farms with tractors, cabins, ferries, roadside petrol stations, offices and flats, grey rain and bright snow, with people in modern clothes such as rain jackets, knitted sweaters, hoodies, suits and work wear. The magic of the tale appears plainly in this everyday world: trolls, talking animals and enchantments look real and slightly absurd, drawn in the same flat style. Every character has their own face, build, age, hair and clothes exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Dry humour, careful composition with one focal point and plenty of negative space. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering.
