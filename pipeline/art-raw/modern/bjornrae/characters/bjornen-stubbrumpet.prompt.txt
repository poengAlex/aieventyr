Character: the Stub-tailed Bear. The same huge, heavy, slow-moving and rather sleepy brown bear after his fishing trip: thick, shaggy dark chocolate-brown winter fur, a broad head with small round ears, small, gentle black eyes under heavy brows, a short greyish-tan muzzle with a big black nose, a patient, slightly puzzled look and enormous flat paws with blunt claws. His long tail is gone: all that is left is a tiny, round, fluffy stump on his big round bottom.

Story world: Present-day Norway in late November and December, where a bear and a fox live among people and talk. Below a spruce forest lies the Frozen Lake, a small lake frozen since late November, with ice fishers hunched on plastic buckets over their holes. By the shore there is a ploughed gravel parking lot with high snowbanks, a small grocery shop, and on Thursdays a white fish van. Up at the edge of the forest is the Sunny Slope, with one dense spruce at the top. The fox's fish are neatly bundled and packed in a plastic bag. The butter is one round yellow plastic tub with a snap-on lid and no readable label. The bear and the fox are wild animals without clothes: they walk on four legs, but can sit up and use their front paws like hands.

This story's season, light and colours: Late November to just before Christmas: short, grey-blue days over a white frozen lake, with the warm orange glow of the shop window and headlights in the parking lot; then a clear, cold December day of low, golden sun on the snowy slope and long blue shadows. Accents: the fox's flame red, butter yellow and dark spruce green.

Art style: Contemporary Scandinavian editorial illustration: flat, graphic shapes with subtle risograph grain and a little hand-drawn texture, clean confident lines and a limited palette of spot inks led by deep petrol blue, with fjord teal, lingonberry red, honey mustard, sage green, warm paper off-white and ink charcoal, like the pages of a risograph-printed book, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, in the cool, clear Nordic light of today. Present-day Norway: fjord towns, suburbs, farms with tractors, cabins, ferries, roadside petrol stations, offices and flats, grey rain and bright snow, with people in modern clothes such as rain jackets, knitted sweaters, hoodies, suits and work wear. The magic of the tale appears plainly in this everyday world: trolls, talking animals and enchantments look real and slightly absurd, drawn in the same flat style. Every character has their own face, build, age, hair and clothes exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Dry humour, careful composition with one focal point and plenty of negative space. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
