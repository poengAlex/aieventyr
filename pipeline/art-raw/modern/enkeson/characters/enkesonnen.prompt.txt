Character: the Widow's Son. A slim, kind-faced young man of about nineteen with milk-pale skin and bright red cheeks, an oval face, a neat, narrow nose, big dark-brown eyes under thick level black eyebrows and short, glossy blue-black hair, wavy on top, with a stubborn cowlick sticking up at the crown. He wears a faded cornflower-blue quilted vest over an oatmeal hoodie, worn dark-grey jeans and scuffed brown boots, with a small, battered red canvas shoulder bag slung across his chest.

Story world: Present-day inland Norway: spruce forest, bogs, a national road and the odd petrol station. The Stranger's house lies at the end of a long gravel road, behind spruce so tall and dense that it is dusk at noon: a big old wooden house, half renovated, with new windows in one wing, plastic sheeting over the other, and paint buckets and ladders along a hallway that ends in four doors set close together. Behind the doors wait a whip braided from wild-rose twigs with thorns as long as drawing pins, a boulder the size of a loaf and an old tin army canteen, a trapdoor over a huge copper cauldron that boils with no heat under it, and a stable built inside the house. The ointment is grey and cold, in a small blue clay jar. A huge hollow linden stands in a green forest clearing near the royal estate.

This story's season, light and colours: Damp, overcast late summer in the inland forests: deep blue-green spruce, rust-red and ochre bog, wild roses and red rosehips, silver drizzle and low, cool golden sunrises. Inside the Stranger's house: flat grey light through plastic sheeting and the warm glow of copper. The royal estate is cool glass, pale stone, clipped green hedges and black cars. Accents: wild-rose pink, rust orange and crisp white.
