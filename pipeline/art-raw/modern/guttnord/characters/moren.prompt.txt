Create a character model sheet for an illustrated book of modern fairy tales. Show the same character three times side by side at the same scale: a front view, a three-quarter view and a side view, each full body from head to feet, standing in a natural, relaxed pose. Plain warm off-white background with a soft shadow on the ground. No scenery and no other characters. Only the props the character's description names, worn or held. Nothing else on the sheet: no separate drawings of objects, tools or close-ups beside the figures, even when the story world mentions them. The three figures are spread evenly across the whole width. Character: the Mother. A thin, worn-out woman of about sixty, a little stooped from her aching hips, with a long, gentle, deeply lined face, tired grey eyes, a thin mouth and one eyebrow often raised in doubt. Her grey hair hangs in one thin braid over her shoulder. She wears a faded plum-coloured fleece top, a darned brown knitted cardigan, grey tracksuit trousers and felt slippers.

Story world: Present-day Norway in raw late autumn. The boy and his mother live in a small flat in a grey block of flats with open-air galleries along each floor, a storage shed at the end of the gallery, and a courtyard and car park between the blocks. A long bus ride north ends at a lonely road junction with a roadside motel and café, its red and blue neon glowing in the wet dark, rubbish containers at the back and a small goat pen behind the car park. From the last bus stop a road runs half an hour out onto a bare headland above the sea, to the North Wind's low grey house, lashed to the rock with steel cables. The oat flour comes in plastic bags. The magic cloth is a plain square white tablecloth. The stick is an old wooden walking stick with a curved handle.

This story's season, light and colours: Raw November: wet asphalt, gusts of sleet, heavy slate-grey skies, a white-capped grey sea off the headland and early, blue-black darkness. Indoors, warm yellow lamplight; at the motel, red and blue neon reflected in puddles. Accents: flour white, neon red, petrol blue and mustard.

Art style: Contemporary Scandinavian editorial illustration: flat, graphic shapes with subtle risograph grain and a little hand-drawn texture, clean confident lines and a limited palette of spot inks led by deep petrol blue, with fjord teal, lingonberry red, honey mustard, sage green, warm paper off-white and ink charcoal, like the pages of a risograph-printed book, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, in the cool, clear Nordic light of today. Present-day Norway: fjord towns, suburbs, farms with tractors, cabins, ferries, roadside petrol stations, offices and flats, grey rain and bright snow, with people in modern clothes such as rain jackets, knitted sweaters, hoodies, suits and work wear. The magic of the tale appears plainly in this everyday world: trolls, talking animals and enchantments look real and slightly absurd, drawn in the same flat style. Every character has their own face, build, age, hair and clothes exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Dry humour, careful composition with one focal point and plenty of negative space. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.

Rules:
- No text of any kind: no letters, words, numbers, signatures, captions, speech bubbles, logos, signs or readable screens. Phones and screens show only light or simple shapes.
- Every named character must match their model sheet and description exactly: the same face, hair, colours, clothes and proportions.
- Suitable for readers of all ages: no blood, wounds or gore, and no killing shown. Show danger and cruelty through atmosphere, posture and suspense, never through injury.
