Character: the Queen. A warm, motherly queen of about forty-five with a round face, a soft double chin, a small snub nose, kind hazel eyes, rosy cheeks and wavy chestnut hair going grey at the temples, pinned up in a low bun. She wears a long plum-purple wool coat with a white faux-fur collar over a dark evening dress, a string of pearls, white leather gloves and low black boots.

Story world: Present-day Norway. The palace has its own chapel and a big old kitchen. The thirteen silver christening spoons all have the same pattern. As wild ducks, the twelve princes are mallard drakes with glossy bottle-green heads, white neck rings, chestnut breasts and grey backs; as young men they look alike, pale, with tousled raven-black hair, and the eldest is the tallest, with a short black beard. Their cabin lies deep in a vast forest near a great bog. Bog cotton grows in fluffy white tufts, and the caps, shirts and scarves sewn from it are pure white. Behind the garage of the big old white official residence stands a late prime minister's reptile house: a long, low glasshouse with terrariums of snakes and toads under green heat lamps. The three children are two small boys with fair curls and a baby girl with black hair.

This story's season, light and colours: Many seasons in one tale, with pure white, blood red and raven black: a snowy winter evening of glittering streetlights and orange snowplough lights at the start; a grey April dawn in the palace kitchen; deep spruce green and a warm wood-stove glow in the cabin; the bog at sunrise and in bright July, white with bog cotton; dark nights at the residence under the sickly green glow of heat lamps; and the harsh white of floodlights and camera flashes on wet slush at the climax.

Present-day Norway: fjord towns, suburbs, farms with tractors, cabins, ferries, roadside petrol stations, offices and flats, grey rain and bright snow, with people in modern clothes such as rain jackets, knitted sweaters, hoodies, suits and work wear.
