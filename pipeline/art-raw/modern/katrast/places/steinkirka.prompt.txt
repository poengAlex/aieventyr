A dry-stone wall surrounds the churchyard, a few birches with yellowing leaves stand around it, and a gravel path leads up to the porch past a small gravel car park.

Story world: Present-day Norway. Kari's father, the king, lives in a palace of glass and concrete at the end of a long avenue of trees, half home and half offices, with garages, a staff car park and a red barn for the royal cows, which graze on the mountain behind. The blue-grey ox keeps a small, folded white cloth in his left ear; spread on the grass, it sets itself with food. Far beyond the last motorways, power lines and paths lie three magic forests: one of copper, one of silver with leaves as thin as razor blades, and one of gold that drips from the leaves. Each troll wears a curved cow horn of healing salve at its belt. At the journey's end, a dark cliff on a windswept plateau looks down on another kingdom's castle with towers and an old stone church. The gold shoes are small, pointed and shining.

This story's season, light and colours: Crisp early autumn: high, pale-blue skies over grey concrete and glass, yellowing birches and purple heather on the windswept plateau, with the ox's glossy blue-grey as the strongest colour until the forests blaze in copper, frost-silver and molten gold. The castle kitchen lies under flat fluorescent light; the church Sundays are bright and windy.
