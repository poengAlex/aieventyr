Character: the Father. A short, stocky, bow-legged old farmer of about seventy with a stiff back, a broad, weather-beaten, wrinkled face, a big bulbous nose, small eyes under shaggy grey eyebrows and a thick, bristly grey beard cut short and square. He wears reading glasses far down his nose, a patched grey-blue wool cardigan with horn buttons over a checked flannel shirt, brown work trousers with braces and thick grey wool socks.

Story world: Present-day Norway. A small, run-down farm with a red farmhouse, a barn and a cramped kitchen with an old wood stove, where the letters from the bank pile up on the table. Up the hillside behind lies the farm's forest plot, steep and dark with old spruce, with a gravel forest road below it. The troll lives inside the mountain above, behind a stone door that slides aside. Inside, everything is far too big: a fireplace you could park a tractor in, benches as high as Askeladden's chest, a table made of one single stone slab, two iron buckets as big as barrels and a porridge pot deeper than he is tall. Askeladden's cheese is a small, round, soft white fresh cheese in baking paper. The chainsaws are two old orange chainsaws.

This story's season, light and colours: A grey, damp October: dark spruce, rust-orange birch leaves, wet green moss and cool grey mist on the hillside, with blue dusk falling early between the trunks. Inside the mountain: warm, flickering firelight on rough grey stone. In the farmhouse kitchen at night: one warm lamp over the table and gold glittering in its light.

Present-day Norway: fjord towns, suburbs, farms with tractors, cabins, ferries, roadside petrol stations, offices and flats, grey rain and bright snow, with people in modern clothes such as rain jackets, knitted sweaters, hoodies, suits and work wear. The magic of the tale appears plainly in this everyday world: trolls, talking animals and enchantments look real and slightly absurd, drawn in the same flat style.
