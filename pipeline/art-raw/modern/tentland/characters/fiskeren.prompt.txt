Character: the Fisherman. A lean, wiry fisherman of about forty-five with a long, weather-beaten face, deep laughter lines, a sharp nose, kind, crinkly blue eyes and a long, drooping black moustache but no beard. He wears a round, brimless old sealskin cap, a thick grey wool jumper, orange oilskin bib trousers and white rubber boots.

Story world: Present-day Norway. The fisherman's small white house stands on the quay of a fjord village below the royal residence, a big modern house behind a tall fence with guards and cameras. His boat is a small white fibreglass fishing boat with an outboard motor and a pair of oars. Far away lies Whiteland, which is on no map: a beach of sand so white it looks fake, between two steep dark mountains, and beyond the trees a low white castle with blue light strips along its roofs. The wishing ring is slim and shiny like a fitness ring; the tiny ring the queen fastens in his hair is engraved. The magic hat, cloak and boots are a tall grey felt hat, a long dark-grey hooded cloak and tall brown boots, and the magic skis are old wooden skis with leather bindings.

This story's season, light and colours: A dark October on the fjord at the start: slate-grey water, wet quays, early dusk and yellow harbour lights. Whiteland is dazzling and unreal: brochure-white sand between steep dark mountains, pale turquoise water, a flat white sky and the cold blue glow of light strips, with fresh snow at the wedding. The journey crosses green June country. Accents: ice blue, white and the gold of the rings.

Art style: Contemporary Scandinavian editorial illustration: flat, graphic shapes with subtle risograph grain and a little hand-drawn texture, clean confident lines and a limited palette of spot inks led by deep petrol blue, with fjord teal, lingonberry red, honey mustard, sage green, warm paper off-white and ink charcoal, like the pages of a risograph-printed book, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours, in the cool, clear Nordic light of today. Present-day Norway: fjord towns, suburbs, farms with tractors, cabins, ferries, roadside petrol stations, offices and flats, grey rain and bright snow, with people in modern clothes such as rain jackets, knitted sweaters, hoodies, suits and work wear. The magic of the tale appears plainly in this everyday world: trolls, talking animals and enchantments look real and slightly absurd, drawn in the same flat style. Every character has their own face, build, age, hair and clothes exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Dry humour, careful composition with one focal point and plenty of negative space. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
