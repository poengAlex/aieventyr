Create a setting reference painting for a children's picture book: one wide, clear view of the place described below, in soft daylight. No people, animals or trolls. The picture will be used to keep this place the same in every illustration, so show its shape, buildings, landforms and colours clearly.

Place: the Sunny Slope. A gentle, south-facing clearing on a snowy hillside in the spruce forest, where the low winter sun shines all day: smooth, sparkling snow with a few grey rocks and tufts of dry golden grass poking through, and at its upper edge one dense, dark-green spruce bush whose low branches sweep the snow, leaving a hollow underneath. Tall spruces and birches ring the clearing, with blue hills far away.

Story world: A snowy forest in old Norway, long ago, where the animals can talk. Below the spruce woods lies the Frozen Lake, a small forest lake covered with thick, snow-dusted ice. Up the hill is the Sunny Slope, an open clearing where the low winter sun shines, with one dense, dark spruce bush at its upper edge. The fox's bundle of fish is five plump silver fish tied together by their tails with a piece of string. The butter tub is a small, round wooden keg with a flat lid and two bent-wood hoops, about as tall as the fox, full of bright-yellow butter. The bear and the fox are natural wild animals without clothes: they walk on four legs, but can sit up and use their front paws like hands.

This story's season, light and colours: Deep midwinter in bright, dazzling sunshine: sparkling white snow, a clear turquoise-blue sky, cool blue shadows and hoarfrost glittering on every twig, with the frozen lake a pale grey-blue. Near Christmas the Sunny Slope basks in warm lemon-yellow sunshine, and the last picture is a clear, deep-blue evening with a lemon glow low in the sky and the first stars. Accents: the fox's flame red, butter yellow and dark spruce green.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.

Rules:
- No text of any kind: no letters, words, numbers, signatures, captions, speech bubbles or logos.
- Every named character must match their model sheet and description exactly: the same face, hair, colours, clothes and proportions.
- Gentle and safe for young children: no blood, wounds or gore, and no violence in progress. Show danger with mood, posture and distance, never with injury.
- Draw each named character only once.
- Show exactly the number of people, animals and things the description asks for, and no extra people.
- One clear moment that reads at a glance, as a single full-bleed picture with no border, frame or comic panels.
