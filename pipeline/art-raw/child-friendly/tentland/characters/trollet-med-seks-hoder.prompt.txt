Character: the Six-Headed Troll. A tall, lanky troll about three times as tall as a man, with sooty black skin, a big white belly like a puffin's, long thin arms and big orange feet. It has exactly six small, round heads on long, thin necks, three growing from each shoulder like two bunches of flowers; each head has a big, bright orange nose, small white eyes with black dots and a tuft of white hair sticking straight up. It wears a short grey wool kilt.

Story world: Old Norway by the sea, long ago. The fisherman's small grey cottage with a turf roof stands on the shore right next to the king's castle, and his boat is a small, tarred, dark-brown rowing boat with two oars. Far across the great sea lies Hvittenland, whose beach is sand as white as milk, with the trolls' gloomy grey castle on a hill behind it and two lions at its gate. The queen's own castle in Hvittenland is white, with a stone stair up to its door. The trolls' birch switches are bundles of thin, bare birch twigs. The queen's wish ring is a plain gold ring, and the little ring she ties into his hair is a tiny gold ring on a blue thread. The magic hat, cloak and boots are a tall grey felt hat, a long dark-grey hooded cloak and a pair of tall brown boots.

This story's season, light and colours: The bright, cold light of early spring in the far north: a copper-red sunset over the sea at the start, then the dazzling milk-white beach of Hvittenland under a pale pearl-blue sky, with turquoise shallows and pale lemon sunrises. The tickling nights happen in near darkness, lit by one warm candle. On the long journey: deep snow still on blue mountains, a steel-grey cold sea and wild, racing storm clouds. Accents: the princess's sky blue and the gold of rings and crowns.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
