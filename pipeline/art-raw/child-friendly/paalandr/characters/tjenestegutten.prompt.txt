Character: the Servant Boy. A very skinny, quick farm boy of about thirteen with a mousy-brown pudding-bowl haircut, a narrow, sunburnt face with a pointed chin, a wide, sly grin and quick green eyes that miss nothing. He wears a baggy, faded brick-red shirt and patched brown trousers that are too short for him, so his bony ankles show, and he goes barefoot. His round grey-green knitted cap with a little pompom on top is always either on his head or waving in his hand.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. A small farm on a green hillside: the farmhouse is a dark log house with a grass roof, a stone doorstep and small-paned windows; across the yard stand Pål's smaller, crooked log cottage, the other house, and a big grey barn with a wide double door. Below them a meadow slopes down past a half-built fence of slanting grey poles to Pål's brown field, a little brook and a birch hill. The farmhouse kitchen has a long scrubbed table with benches, an open hearth with a black pot, and a plank door that opens inwards, with room to hide behind it. Rømmegrøt is thick, creamy-white sour-cream porridge in a big round wooden bowl, with a golden pool of melted butter in the middle and cinnamon sugar on top. Lefse is a big, round, thin, soft flatbread.

This story's season, light and colours: Late spring at ploughing time: rich chocolate-brown ploughed earth, birches in bright lime-green new leaf, golden marsh marigolds along the brook, and warm, still sunshine under a clear sky. Indoors: warm hearth firelight and the creamy white of the porridge.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
