Place: the Forest Spring. A small, perfectly clear forest spring in a ring of mossy stones and ferns, still as a mirror, in a quiet clearing at the edge of a great spruce-and-birch forest. A tall old oak with broad, spreading branches leans right over the water. Through the trees on one side, the pale towers and slate-blue roofs of a castle can be seen close by.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, in and around a great, deep forest of spruce and birch. The poor couple live in a tiny log hut far inside the forest. The foster mother's house is a bright, quiet, whitewashed house with pale wooden floors and red geraniums in every window. In its hallway are three small, child-height doors of pale wood side by side, each with a little carved heart. Behind them wait a glittering silver-white star the size of an apple, a big, round, silver-white moon and a small, blazing golden sun. Far across the forest, a clear spring lies below a tall old oak, close to a storybook castle of pale sandstone with steep slate-blue roofs and round towers. The babies are wrapped in white shawls and sleep in a carved wooden cradle.

This story's season, light and colours: Mild, still spring: fresh pale-green birch leaves, white cherry blossom and lilac; pearly-pink dawns with mist over the spring, rose-and-lavender dusks, and deep-indigo nights with soft silver light. The foster mother's house glows creamy white and pale gold. Accents: the silver of the star and the moon, the warm gold of the sun and the deep blue of the foster mother's veil.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
