Place: the Smithy. The inside of a small, sooty log smithy with a low beamed ceiling: a brick forge with a hood and glowing coals, a big leather bellows beside it, a black anvil on a thick tree stump in the middle, hammers, tongs and horseshoes hanging on the dark log walls, a wooden water barrel by the wall, a three-legged stool by the forge, and a big, squashy armchair of faded rose-red plush in the corner. Wide double doors open onto bright sunshine.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. The smith's smithy is a squat log building with a grass-covered turf roof, soot-blackened walls and wide double doors, standing beside a dusty country road. A gnarled old pear tree, heavy with ripe yellow pears, grows against its sunny side wall. The wire purse is a small, round coin purse of woven silver-grey steel wire, the size of a child's hand, with a snap clasp. The smith's big hammer is a heavy iron sledgehammer with a long ash handle. Far along the road, the way splits in two: one road climbs a green hill into soft white clouds to the gate of heaven, a tall arched gate of white-painted wood with gold hinges; the other dips into a smoky, rocky gully to the Devil's gate, a tall, crooked, soot-black iron gate with nine big iron locks in a row and one huge padlock.

This story's season, light and colours: A heatwave in the hottest days of summer: glaring white-gold sunlight, parched straw-yellow grass, an ochre dust road shimmering in the heat and ripe yellow pears among dusty green leaves. Inside the smithy: soot-black and charcoal greys lit by glowing ember-orange and showers of bright sparks. At the gate of heaven: soft white clouds and pale gold light; at the Devil's gate: a smoky plum-purple haze.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
