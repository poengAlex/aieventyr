Character: the Farmer's Wife. A small, wiry, bustling woman of about forty-five with a narrow, freckled face, a long pointed nose, bright little grey eyes, a busy, pursed mouth and frizzy ginger hair escaping from a patched brown headscarf. She wears a patched mud-brown skirt, a faded grey-blue blouse and a ragged grey shawl. On her right foot is one brand-new, glossy black leather shoe with a big silver buckle; on her left foot, an old, holey grey felt slipper.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, in late winter. The poor little farm is a single tiny, lopsided cottage of grey, weathered logs with a sagging, snow-covered turf roof, a crooked chimney, one small window and a low door. Inside is one cramped room with a bare plank floor, a rough little table, a wobbly bench along the wall, two rickety stools and a three-legged stool, a small stone fireplace in the corner with a sooty kettle, and one shelf with two cracked clay bowls. Almost everything is old, grey and patched. The only new things in the house are the farmer's one new sleeve, the wife's one new shoe and the daughter's new cap. The suitor carries a small, square brown leather travel bag with a brass clasp.

This story's season, light and colours: Late winter on a frosty February afternoon: old, crusted snow with lilac-blue shadows, long icicles on the eaves and a low, pink sun. Inside the cottage: warm orange firelight and smoky brown shadows, from which the family's three new things shine out: a bright lingonberry-red sleeve, a glossy black shoe with a silver buckle and an emerald-green cap.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
