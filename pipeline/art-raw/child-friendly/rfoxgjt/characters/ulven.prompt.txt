Character: the Wolf. A lanky, long-legged grey wolf with shaggy smoke-grey fur, darker along his back and pale silver on his chest and legs, a long narrow muzzle, pale yellow eyes, tall pointed ears with the left one bent over at the tip, a thin bushy tail held low, and a polite, awkward, closed-mouth smile. He looks shy and gangly rather than fierce.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, where the animals can talk. The farm wife spends the summer at her mountain summer farm, high up where the birch forest ends: a small grey log hut with a grass roof and a stone doorstep, a little cowshed, and a split-rail fence around a green mountain pasture, with a clear, stony brook below and a little grove of birches beyond it. A grassy track leads from the hut down into the birch forest. Her animals are exactly two white goats, three woolly white sheep and two brown-and-white cows. Her butter churn is a tall, narrow wooden churn with dark hoops and a long wooden dasher sticking out of its lid. The wolf and the fox are natural animals, apart from the fox's little pouch.

This story's season, light and colours: Late August at a mountain summer farm, when the fells take on their first autumn colours: carpets of deep-red dwarf birch and golden grass on the slopes, ripe blueberries, blue ridges far away, and clear, cool, sunny air. The evening scene glows orange and rose over the mountains. Accents: dwarf-birch red, the fox's golden orange and creamy white.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
