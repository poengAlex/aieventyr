Place: the Cottage Kitchen. A small, low log room with dark, smoke-stained walls and a ceiling of rough beams. A stone fireplace with a black cooking pot glows in the corner, a plain wooden table stands under a small frosted window, a bench runs along the wall, and a broom and a water bucket stand by the door. A worn rag rug lies on the floorboards. Poor, but tidy and cosy.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, in the first cold weeks of winter. The boy and his mother live in a small, grey, weathered log cottage with a turf roof; beside it stands a little stabbur, a storehouse on posts with a steep wooden stair up to its door. Far away, on top of a high, snowy mountain with a few wind-bent spruces, lives the North Wind. By the road home lies the inn, a big, low log house with a lantern at the door and a goat shed behind. The magic cloth is a square white linen tablecloth with a red woven zigzag border, and the swapped one looks exactly the same. The stick is a knobbly old stick of pale birch wood, about as long as the boy is tall. The flour is carried in a small grey sack.

This story's season, light and colours: The first cold weeks of winter: a thin, new layer of snow on frozen ground, hoarfrost on every twig, blustery wind and cold blue-grey skies with fast grey clouds; deep snow on the North Wind's mountain. Indoors: warm, golden firelight and candlelight against the cold blue light at the windows.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
