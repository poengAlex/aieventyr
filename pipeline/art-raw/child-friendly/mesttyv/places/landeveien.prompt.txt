Place: the Forest Road. A narrow, dusty dirt road winding through a light forest of white-trunked birches and dark spruces, with a grassy ditch along one side, big mossy grey stones and round green bushes at the roadside, and a weathered grey wooden rail fence where the road bends. Here and there a big old birch leans over the road with a thick, low branch.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. The cottager's tiny, crooked turf-roofed hut stands alone at the edge of the woods. Deep in a dark spruce forest lies the robbers' big, dim timber house with a stone fireplace. The farmer walks his three oxen, big and gentle, reddish-brown with white faces and short curved horns, along the Forest Road. The Bailiff lives at the Manor Farm, with a goose house full of white geese and a long stable of twelve stalls for his twelve dun fjord horses with cream-and-black manes; he himself rides a big dapple-grey horse. The priest lives at the vicarage, with a huge old linden tree in the garden. The master thief's sack is a big patched brown sack, and the barrel is a big oak barrel on a small two-wheeled cart pulled by a bony old brown mare.

This story's season, light and colours: A blustery, rain-washed late spring: a stormy blue-black night at the start, then fresh leaf-green birches, apple blossom and tulips at the Manor Farm, and dusty, sunny country roads by day. Many of the tricks happen at dusk or at night, in deep indigo moonlight with warm amber lantern and candle glow, and the mornings come up rosy pink. Accent colours: the thief's bottle-green, brass and blossom pink.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
