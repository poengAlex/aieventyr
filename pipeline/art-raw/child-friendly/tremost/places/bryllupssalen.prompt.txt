Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. A poor hunter lives in a small log cabin deep in a spruce forest. The royal farm is a big, friendly cluster of honey-brown log buildings with grass roofs, carved gables and small-paned windows around a cobbled yard with a well. The flax is soft, pale-gold fibre in a big wicker basket; spun, it becomes smooth, shiny golden yarn wound into soft balls; woven, it becomes a thick roll of fine cream-white linen; sewn, it becomes a neat pile of white linen shirts with shiny buttons. The Queen's servant girls are three young women in grey dresses, white aprons and white caps. The wedding is held in the royal farm's great hall, with six guests in festive clothes.

Indoors: warm candle and lamp light on flax-gold yarn, cream linen and soft wool colours. The wedding glows with candlelight, bowls of pink and white garden flowers and a tall cake. Accents: flax gold and the Queen's rainbow-striped knitting.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours.
