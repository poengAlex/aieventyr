Character: the Maiden. A slim girl of about seventeen with snow-pale skin, rosy-red cheeks and lips, a soft oval face, a small straight nose and large, dark-grey eyes with long black lashes. Her raven-black hair hangs in one thick, loose braid over her shoulder, tied with a white ribbon. She wears a plain dark-teal wool dress with a deep front pocket, where the handle of a small silver spoon peeks out, a long grey knitted shawl and thin, worn brown shoes.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. The queen's castle lies among wide, snowy fields, with a grey split-rail fence along the sleigh road; her sleigh is small and red with brass bells, pulled by one brown horse. The thirteen silver spoons are alike, each with a tiny crown on the handle. As wild ducks, the twelve brothers are mallard drakes with glossy bottle-green heads, white neck rings, chestnut breasts and grey backs. As men they look alike, with tousled raven-black hair, bottle-green caps and chestnut-brown tunics; the eldest is the tallest and has a short black beard. Bog cotton grows in fluffy white tufts, and everything made from it is soft and snow-white. The old gardener is small and bald with a grey beard; his wife is tall and thin in a white headscarf. The three children are two boys with fair curls and a girl with black hair.

This story's season, light and colours: Snow-white, rose-red and raven-black accents throughout. The opening is a still, hushed winter day of fresh snow under a pale, low sun; the brothers' forest is deep spruce-green with warm amber firelight in the cabin; the bog lies in pearly midsummer fog with the sun glowing through; the castle scenes have cool, dewy early-morning light and pale-green lawns.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
