Character: Vesle Aase. A small, sturdy goose girl of about fifteen, a head shorter than most, with a round, sun-browned face, a small determined chin, a snub nose, calm dark-brown eyes that look straight at you and thick, straight eyebrows. Her thick dark-brown hair is in one short, stubby plait tied with red wool. She wears a plain brown wool dress mended with neat blue and green patches, a crisp white apron, wooden clogs and a little patchwork sewing bag hanging from her apron string.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. The king's castle is a big, white-painted timber palace with carved wooden balconies and blue window frames, set among green meadows beside a dusty country road with grassy ditches. The king has a large flock of geese, big and small, white and grey. Vesle Aase is always sewing a small dress made of patch on patch in many colours. The fine bedchamber has a tall door painted with blue and red rosemaling flowers and a worn wooden threshold, where the truth stone lies at night. The Prince rides a tall bay horse and wears a slim gold ring with a tiny red stone on his little finger, until the night he slips it onto a sleeping girl's finger.

This story's season, light and colours: Late spring in fresh, gentle pastels: bright-green meadows full of golden dandelions and fluffy white dandelion clocks, white and grey geese, a soft sky-blue sky and clear, milky morning light. The night scenes inside the castle are a deep, hushed blue, lit only by a single candle or the truth stone's warm golden glow.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture.
