Character: Our Lord. A very old, tall and slightly stooped wanderer with a long, gentle face, deep-set kind blue-grey eyes, a high wrinkled forehead, thin snow-white hair to his shoulders and a long, flowing white beard that reaches his belt. He wears a plain, ankle-length hooded brown homespun robe tied with a rope, and simple sandals over woollen foot-wraps. He leans on a tall, knobbly wooden staff. A humble traveller with no halo.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, in the days when Our Lord and St Peter wandered the earth. Gjertrud's home is a small, low log cottage with a thick snow-covered turf roof and a squat grey stone chimney, alone at the edge of a snowy spruce forest. Inside is one warm, firelit room: an open stone hearth with a big, round, flat iron griddle over the embers, a long wooden baking board dusted with flour, a rolling pin, a wooden bench by the door, and a tall stack of lefser, which are big, thin, round, soft flatbreads, pale with brown speckles. Every lefse she rolls, however tiny the lump of dough, grows on the griddle until it is far too big.

This story's season, light and colours: A hushed early-winter evening in the blue hour: big, soft snowflakes drifting down, thick snow on roofs and spruces, and everything outside in deep, velvety blues. Inside the cottage: warm amber firelight, golden-brown lefser and pale flour. The bright scarlet of the red cap is the one strong accent colour, later joined by the bird's sooty black.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
