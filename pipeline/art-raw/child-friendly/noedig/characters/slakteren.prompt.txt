Character: the Butcher. A huge, barrel-chested butcher of about fifty with a round, shiny red face, a bulbous nose, twinkling little black eyes, bushy black eyebrows, a sly grin and a big, round, bushy black beard. He wears a small white linen cap, a red shirt with the sleeves rolled up over thick arms and a long white apron with blue stripes, and he always has a string of sausages slung over one shoulder.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago, at sowing time. The Wife and the Husband have a small, poor farm with one brown field. A dusty road leads to a little market town, where the Butcher has an open wooden booth hung with rows of sausages and a bench in front. The Wife's cow is thin and fawn-coloured; her hen is a scrawny black-and-white speckled hen with a floppy red comb and half her feathers missing. Far away along the roads lie a brand-new house of pale, fresh logs with no windows, a small farmhouse, and a big, rich farm. The rich farm's horse is a sturdy blue-grey dappled horse with a black mane and tail; the other horse there is chestnut-brown. The cart is a small, two-wheeled wooden farm cart.

This story's season, light and colours: A dry, sunny spring at sowing time: pale-blue skies with small, round white clouds like grazing sheep, bare brown fields and fresh furrows, birches with their first tiny mouse-ear leaves, and white wood anemones and yellow coltsfoot along the ditches and fences. Crisp, clear, bright light and dusty roads. Accents: the Wife's rust-orange skirt, fluffy white feathers and the blue-grey horse.

Art style: Warm Scandinavian children's picture-book illustration: gouache and watercolour on lightly textured cold-press paper, with soft coloured-pencil outlines and visible brush and paper texture. Rounded, friendly shapes and simple, expressive faces with clear emotions, but every character has their own face shape, nose, eyes, hair, build and age exactly as described, so no two characters look alike. Base palette of moss green, spruce green, lingonberry red, birch white, fjord teal, honey yellow and warm earth browns, shifted towards each story's own season, light and colours. The pictures are the colour plates of a classic printed storybook with warm cream pages, so the lightest tones are warm and creamy, never stark white, and lingonberry red is the accent that comes back in every picture. The picture fills the whole image, with no border, frame or lettering. Costumes and places from old rural Norway: spruce and birch forests, fjords and mountains, turf-roofed log houses, carved wood, rosemaling patterns, wool and linen clothes. Kings and princesses wear simple storybook finery. Trolls are huge, lumpy and mossy and look silly rather than scary. Clear, uncluttered composition with one focal point and room to breathe. Cosy, safe and full of wonder. Not photorealistic, not 3D, not anime, no glossy digital look.
