Character: Rich Per. A tall, bony merchant of about fifty with a long, horsey face, a jutting chin, a sharp hooked nose, small, darting, greedy grey eyes, a thin mouth, bushy salt-and-pepper side-whiskers and slicked-back black hair with a pointed widow's peak. He wears a long wine-red frock coat whose big pockets bulge with rolls of coloured ribbon, a shiny green-and-gold striped silk waistcoat, grey knee breeches, white stockings and buckled shoes, a gold ring on every finger and a fat leather money purse on his belt.

Story world: Old rural Norway, long ago. Rich Per Kremmer lives on a big, prosperous farm; just below it a little grey watermill turns on a brook, and far downstream on the river stands a second, older mill with a big wooden waterwheel. The little chest is a small pine box painted with blue and red rosemaling flowers, with an iron key in its lock. Per's letters are folded papers sealed with red wax. The robbers' farm is a shabby log farmhouse deep in a dark spruce forest. At a wide river far away waits the old ferry: a long, flat-bottomed, tarred black punt with square ends, pushed with a very long wooden pole. On the hill beyond it stands the Dragon's crooked, slate-grey stone castle at Dybenfart. The three feathers are long, shining golden feathers, each as long as a man's arm.

This story's season, light and colours: Late spring along the rivers: fast, high, glittering water, bird-cherry trees foaming with white blossom on the banks, marsh marigolds glowing yellow in the wet meadows, and long rosy-gold sunsets. At night: deep blue shadows and warm lamplight. Inside the Dragon's castle: slate-blue gloom and ember-orange firelight.
