The Rooster and the Hen once went into the Hazelwood to pick nuts; and there the hen got a bit of nutshell stuck in her throat and lay flapping and thrashing her wings. The rooster was to fetch water for her. So he came to the spring and said, “Dear little spring, give me water; the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

The spring answered, “You’ll get no water from me until you bring me leaves.”

So the rooster ran to the linden tree. “Dear linden, give me leaves; the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no leaves from me,” said the linden, “until you bring me red gold ribbons.”

So the rooster ran to the Virgin Mary. “Dear Virgin Mary, give me red gold ribbons; the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no red gold ribbons from me,” said the Virgin Mary, “until you bring me shoes.”

So the rooster ran to the shoemaker. “Dear shoemaker, give me shoes; the shoes I will give to the Virgin Mary, the Virgin Mary will give me red gold ribbons, the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no shoes from me,” said the shoemaker, “until you bring me bristles.”

So the rooster ran to the sow. “Dear sow, give me bristles; the bristles I will give to the shoemaker, the shoemaker will give me shoes, the shoes I will give to the Virgin Mary, the Virgin Mary will give me red gold ribbons, the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no bristles from me,” said the sow, “until you bring me grain.”

So the rooster ran to the wooden man. “Dear wooden man, give me grain; the grain I will give to the sow, the sow will give me bristles, the bristles I will give to the shoemaker, the shoemaker will give me shoes, the shoes I will give to the Virgin Mary, the Virgin Mary will give me red gold ribbons, the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no grain from me,” said the wooden man, “until you bring me lefse.”

So the rooster ran to the baking wife. “Dear baking wife, give me lefse; the lefse I will give to the wooden man, the wooden man will give me grain, the grain I will give to the sow, the sow will give me bristles, the bristles I will give to the shoemaker, the shoemaker will give me shoes, the shoes I will give to the Virgin Mary, the Virgin Mary will give me red gold ribbons, the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no lefse from me,” said the baking wife, “until you bring me firewood.”

So the rooster ran to the woodcutter. “Dear woodcutter, give me firewood; the firewood I will give to the baking wife, the baking wife will give me lefse, the lefse I will give to the wooden man, the wooden man will give me grain, the grain I will give to the sow, the sow will give me bristles, the bristles I will give to the shoemaker, the shoemaker will give me shoes, the shoes I will give to the Virgin Mary, the Virgin Mary will give me red gold ribbons, the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no firewood from me,” said the woodcutter, “until you bring me an axe.”

So the rooster ran to the smith. “Dear smith, give me an axe; the axe I will give to the woodcutter, the woodcutter will give me firewood, the firewood I will give to the baking wife, the baking wife will give me lefse, the lefse I will give to the wooden man, the wooden man will give me grain, the grain I will give to the sow, the sow will give me bristles, the bristles I will give to the shoemaker, the shoemaker will give me shoes, the shoes I will give to the Virgin Mary, the Virgin Mary will give me red gold ribbons, the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

“You’ll get no axe from me,” said the smith, “until you bring me charcoal.”

So the rooster ran to the charcoal burner. “Dear charcoal burner, give me charcoal; the charcoal I will give to the smith, the smith will give me an axe, the axe I will give to the woodcutter, the woodcutter will give me firewood, the firewood I will give to the baking wife, the baking wife will give me lefse, the lefse I will give to the wooden man, the wooden man will give me grain, the grain I will give to the sow, the sow will give me bristles, the bristles I will give to the shoemaker, the shoemaker will give me shoes, the shoes I will give to the Virgin Mary, the Virgin Mary will give me red gold ribbons, the red gold ribbons I will give to the linden, the linden will give me leaves, the leaves I will give to the spring, the spring will give me water, and the water I will give to my darling hen, who lies at death’s door in the Hazelwood.”

Then the charcoal burner took pity on the rooster and gave him charcoal. So the smith got charcoal and the woodcutter an axe, and the baking wife firewood, and the wooden man lefse, and the sow grain, and the shoemaker bristles, and the Virgin Mary shoes, and the linden red gold ribbons, and the spring leaves, and the rooster water; and he gave it to his darling hen, who lay at death’s door in the Hazelwood—then she was well again.
