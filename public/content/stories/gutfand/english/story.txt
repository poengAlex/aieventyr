Once there was a boy walking along the road, cracking nuts as he went. He came upon a worm-eaten one, and at that very moment he met the Devil.

“Is it true,” said the boy, “what people say—that the Devil can make himself as small as he likes and squeeze through the eye of a needle?”

“Yes!” answered the Devil.

“Oh, then let me see it—crawl into this nut!” said the boy, and the Devil did.

But once he had wriggled in through the little worm-hole, the boy jammed a peg into it. “Now I’ve got you there,” he said, and slipped the nut into his pocket.

After he had walked a while, he came to a blacksmith’s forge. In he went and asked the smith if he would smash that nut for him.

“Yes, that’ll be easy enough,” said the blacksmith. He took his smallest hammer, set the nut on the anvil, and struck—but it would not break. Then he took a slightly bigger hammer, but that wasn’t heavy enough either. He took one bigger still, but that did no good; and then the blacksmith grew angry and grabbed the great sledge.

“I’ll smash you, I will,” he said, and brought it down with all his might. Then the nut burst to bits with a crack—half the roof of the forge flew off, and it roared as though the whole hut were coming down.

“I do believe the Devil was in that nut!” cried the blacksmith.

“Yes, he was,” said the boy.
