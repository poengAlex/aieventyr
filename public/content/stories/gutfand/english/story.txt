Once upon a time there was a boy who went along the road cracking nuts. He came upon one that was worm-eaten, and at that very moment he met the Devil.

“Is it true,” said the boy, “what people say, that the Devil can make himself as small as he likes and squeeze through a pinhole?”

“It is!” said the Devil.

“Oh, then let me see you do it. Crawl into this nut,” said the boy.

So the Devil did. He made himself smaller and smaller and wriggled in through the wormhole. The moment he was inside, the boy jammed a little peg into the hole.

“Now I’ve got you,” he said, and put the nut in his pocket.

When he had walked on a while, he came to a smithy. In he went and asked the blacksmith to crack the nut for him.

“Easy enough,” said the blacksmith. He took his smallest hammer, laid the nut on the anvil and struck. But the nut would not break.

So he took a hammer a little bigger. That wasn’t heavy enough either. He took one bigger still, but that did no good. Then the blacksmith lost his temper and took down the great sledgehammer. The boy stood by with his hands in his pockets.

“I’ll have you in pieces yet,” said the blacksmith, and struck with all his might.

The nut burst with such a bang that half the roof flew off the smithy, and the whole place shook as if it were coming down around their ears.

The blacksmith stood staring at the wreckage on the anvil.

“I do believe the Devil was in that nut!” he said.

“So he was,” said the boy.
