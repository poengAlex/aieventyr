In the days when our Lord and Saint Peter went walking and wandering here on earth, they once came to a woman who sat baking. Her name was Gertrud, and she had a red cap on her head. They had walked a long way and were both hungry, so our Lord asked her, as kindly as could be, for a flatbread to taste.

Yes, he should have one, she said; but she took a tiny little bit of dough and rolled it out—yet it grew so large it covered the whole griddle. No, that flatbread was too big; he could not have it.

She took an even smaller bit; but when she had baked it out and spread it on the griddle, that flatbread too was too big; he could not have that one either. The third time she took a smaller bit still, a speck of a thing; but this time as well the flatbread became far too large.

“Then I have nothing to give you,” said Gertrud. “You may as well go again without so much as a taste, for the flatbreads all get too big.”

Then our Lord grew angry and said, “Because you begrudged me so sorely, you shall have this punishment: you shall become a bird, and take your dry food from between bark and wood, and you shall have nothing to drink except when it rains.”

And scarcely had he spoken the last word when she became the Gertrud-bird and flew from the baking loft straight up through the chimney. And to this very day you can see her flying about with her red cap and black all over her body, for the soot from the chimney stained her. She pecks and taps at the trees unceasingly for food, and she pipes for rainy weather; for she is always thirsty, and then she waits for drink.
