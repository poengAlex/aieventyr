Once upon a time there was a woman who set out to hire herself a herdsman, and before long she met a bear.

“Where are you off to?” said the Bear.

“Oh, I’m going out to hire myself a herdsman,” said the woman.

“Won’t you have me for your herdsman?” asked the Bear.

“Yes, if you can call the herd, that is,” said the woman.

The Bear filled his lungs. “Hurr—eel!” he bellowed, so that the trees shook.

“No, I won’t have you,” said the woman, gripping her stick, and she went on her way.

When she had gone a bit farther, she met a wolf.

“Where are you off to?” said the Wolf.

“Oh, I’m going out to hire myself a herdsman,” said the woman.

“Won’t you have me for your herdsman?” asked the Wolf.

“Yes, if you can call the herd, that is,” said the woman.

The Wolf lifted his muzzle. “Ooo—ooo!” he howled, and it sent a shiver down her back.

“No, I won’t have you,” said the woman, and waved him off the road.

When she had gone a bit farther still, she met a fox.

“Where are you off to?” said the Fox.

“Oh, I’m going out to hire myself a herdsman,” said the woman.

“Won’t you have me for your herdsman?” asked the Fox.

“Yes, if you can call the herd, that is,” said the woman.

The Fox sat up straight. “Dil—dal—holom!” he sang, so clear and sweet.

The woman’s face lit up. “Yes, you’re the one I’ll have,” she said, and she set the Fox to herd her animals.

The first day the Fox herded, he ate up all the woman’s goats. The second day he finished off all her sheep. And the third day he ate up all her cows.

That evening he came home alone, licking his lips, and the woman asked what he had done with all her animals.

“The skull’s in the stream, and the body’s in the wood,” said the Fox.

The woman was standing at her churn, making butter. But she thought she had better go and look for the animals all the same, and while she was gone, the Fox slipped down into the churn and ate up the cream.

When she came back and saw what he had done, she was so angry that she snatched up the last little bit of cream and flung it at him. It caught him on the very tip of his tail.

And that is why the fox has a white tip to his tail.
