The Bear and the Fox

Why the Bear Has a Stumpy Tail

Once the Bear met the Fox, who came sneaking along with a bundle of fish he had stolen.

“Where did you get that?” asked the Bear.

“I’ve been out fishing, Master Bear!” answered the Fox.

Then the Bear took a fancy to learning how to fish too, and begged the Fox to tell him how he ought to go about it.

“It’s a simple art for you,” said the Fox, “and you’ll learn it in no time. You only have to go out on the ice, chop a hole, and stick your tail down into it, and then you must hold it there a good long while. Don’t pay any mind if it stings a bit—that’s when the fish are biting. The longer you can hold it there, the more fish you’ll get. And before you know it, you must give it a sharp jerk upward!”

Yes, the Bear did just as the Fox had told him, and held his tail down in the hole for a long, long time, until it froze fast. Then he gave it a sharp jerk—right off it came, and ever since that day he has gone about with a stumpy tail.

The Fox Tricks the Bear Out of the Christmas Butter

Once the Bear and the Fox had bought a tub of butter together. They meant to have it for Christmas, so they hid it under a thick spruce bush. Then they went a little way off and lay down in a sunny slope to sleep. After they had lain there a while, the Fox got up and cried, “Yes!”—and off he went, straight to the butter tub, and ate a good third of it. But when he came back and the Bear asked where he had been, since his muzzle looked so greasy, he said, “Would you believe it, I was invited to a christening.”

“Oh, so!” said the Bear. “What did they name the child?”

“Just-Started,” said the Fox.

Then they lay down to sleep again. In a little while the Fox sprang up once more and cried, “Yes!” and away he darted to the butter tub. That time he ate a fine big lump too. When he came back and the Bear again asked where he had been, he answered, “Ah—wasn’t I invited to another christening now, do you think?”

“And what did they name the child this time?” asked the Bear.

“Half-Eaten,” said the Fox.

The Bear thought it was a strange name, but he didn’t wonder over it for long before he yawned and fell asleep again; and it wasn’t long before it went the same as both the other times: the Fox jumped up again, cried “Yes!” and off he went to the butter tub, and that time he ate the whole rest of it. When he came back, he had been at yet another christening; and when the Bear wanted to know what the child was called, the Fox answered, “Licked-Clean-at-the-Bottom!”

Then they lay down again and slept a good while. After that they were going to go and look after the butter; but it was all eaten up. So the Bear blamed the Fox and the Fox blamed the Bear; each said the other had been off at the butter tub while he himself lay sleeping.

“Well, well,” said Reynard, “we’ll soon find out which of us stole the butter. Now we’ll lie down over there in the sunny slope: the one who’s greasiest at the rear end when we wake up—he’s the one who stole it.”

Yes, the Bear was quite willing to agree to that test, and since he knew in his own mind he hadn’t even tasted the butter, he lay down in the sun and went to sleep without a worry. Then Reynard slunk off to the tub for a butter-scraping that still clung in a crack, and back to the Bear, and smeared it on his backside; then he lay down to sleep as if nothing at all had happened. And when they both woke, the sun had melted the butter, and so the Bear was the one who had eaten the butter after all—so it seemed.
