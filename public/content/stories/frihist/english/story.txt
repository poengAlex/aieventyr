Once there was a lad who was out courting. Among other places he came to a farm where the people lived in sheer poverty and want; but when the suitor arrived, they were bound to seem well-to-do—anyone can understand that.

The man had got himself a new sleeve on his sweater. “Sit down!” he said to the suitor. “But it’s so dusty everywhere!” And off he went, rubbing and wiping the benches and tables with that new sweater-sleeve of his, while he kept the other arm tucked behind his back.

The wife had got herself one new shoe; with it she went about prying and kicking at everything—stools and chairs. “There’s so much in the way, it’s such a mess in here!” she said.

Then they called to their daughter, telling her to come and tidy up. She had got herself a new cap; so she only poked her head through the doorway and nodded this way and that. “I can’t be everywhere at once, can I?” she said.

Oh yes—prosperous folk indeed, the suitor had come to visit.
