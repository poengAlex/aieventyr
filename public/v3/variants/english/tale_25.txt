THE DOLL IN THE GRASS

Once upon a time there was a King who had twelve sons. When they grew up, he told them they should travel out into the world to find themselves wives. But each bride must be able to spin, weave, and sew a shirt all in one day, otherwise he would not accept her as a daughter-in-law. To each son he gave a horse and a new set of armor, and so they set off into the world to look for wives. But once they had gone a little way, they decided that The Ash Lad was not allowed to come along, because he was useless at everything. So The Ash Lad had to stay behind; there was nothing he could do about it, and he did not know what to do or where to turn. He was so downcast that he got off his horse and sat down in the grass to cry.

After he had sat there for a little while, one of the small mounds in the grass began to stir, and out of it came a tiny white figure. As it came closer, The Ash Lad saw that it was a lovely little girl, but she was so extremely small. She walked up to him and asked if he would like to come down and see The Doll in the Grass. Yes, he would, and so he did.

Once he got down there, The Doll in the Grass sat on a chair; she looked so beautiful and so finely dressed, and she asked The Ash Lad where he was going and what business he was on.

He replied that there were twelve brothers in all, and that the King had given them horses and armor and told them to go out into the world to find wives who could spin, weave, and sew a shirt in one day. “But if you would do that,” The Ash Lad said to The Doll in the Grass, “and you would become my wife, then I will travel no further.” Yes, she would gladly do it, and she hurried to spin, weave, and sew the shirt. But it turned out so strikingly small, no bigger than this—

The Ash Lad took it home. When he brought it to the King, he felt shy because it was so little. Even so, the King said he would have her as a daughter-in-law, and The Ash Lad, happy and content, went back to fetch his little sweetheart. When he arrived at The Doll in the Grass, he wanted to lift her up on his horse in front of him. But no, she refused. She said she wanted to sit and ride in a silver spoon, and she already had two tiny white horses to pull her. So off they went, he on his horse and she in the silver spoon, and the two little white horses pulling her were actually two small white mice. But The Ash Lad always kept to the other side of the road, because he was so afraid he might accidentally ride over her, she was so very small.

After they had gone a way, they came to a large lake. The Ash Lad’s horse got skittish, swerved over to the other side of the road, and tipped over the spoon, so The Doll in the Grass fell into the water. The Ash Lad was so upset, he did not know how he would get her out again. But after a moment a merman came up with her, and now she was as big as any other grown person—and much more beautiful than before. Then he sat her in front of him on the horse and rode home.

By the time The Ash Lad arrived back, all his brothers had also returned, each with his sweetheart. But they were so ugly and dreadful, and they had argued and tussled with their sweethearts on the way. On their heads, they wore hats painted with tar and soot, and it had run off the hats and down over their faces, making them even uglier and more frightful. When the brothers saw The Ash Lad’s sweetheart, they all became jealous of him. But the King was so pleased with The Ash Lad and his bride that he sent all the others away. Then The Ash Lad celebrated his wedding with The Doll in the Grass, and after that they lived happily and well for a very, very long time. And if they have not died, they live there still.
