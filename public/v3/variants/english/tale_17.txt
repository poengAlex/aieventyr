THE BEAR AND THE FOX

Why the Bear Has a Stumpy Tail

One day, the Bear met the Fox, who came sneaking along with a bundle of fish he had stolen.

“Where did you get that from?” asked the Bear.

“I’ve been out fishing, Mr. Bear!” replied the Fox.

Then the Bear took a notion to learn how to fish, and asked the Fox how he should go about it.

“It’s a simple art for someone like you,” answered the Fox, “and it’s quickly learned. You just go out on the ice, chop a hole, and stick your tail in; then you must keep it there for quite a while. Don’t worry if it stings your tail a bit—that just means the fish are biting. The longer you keep it there, the more fish you’ll catch. And then, all at once, you must yank it up!”

Yes, the Bear did just as the Fox had told him, keeping his tail down in the hole for a long, long time, until it froze fast. Then he gave it a sudden pull—off it came, and that’s why he goes around with a stumpy tail to this very day.

The Fox Tricks the Bear Out of the Christmas Food

Once, the Bear and the Fox bought a butter tub together, which they were planning to share at Christmas. They hid it beneath a thick spruce bush. Then the two went a little way off and lay down on a sunny slope to sleep.

After they had been lying there for a while, the Fox got up and shouted, “Yes!”—and ran straight to the butter tub, where he ate a good third of it. When he returned, the Bear, noticing how greasy the Fox’s mouth was, asked where he had been.

“Don’t you believe I was invited to a baby shower?” replied the Fox.

“Oh really! And what was the baby’s name?” asked the Bear.

“Started-On,” said the Fox.

They settled down to sleep again. After a short time, the Fox sprang up once more and shouted, “Yes!” and hurried off to the butter tub again, where he ate another large portion. When he got back, and the Bear asked where he had gone this time, he answered:

“Well, wouldn’t you know it, I got invited to another baby shower!”

“What was the baby’s name this time?” asked the Bear.

“Half-Eaten,” said the Fox.

The Bear thought the name sounded odd, but he didn’t think long about it before he yawned and drifted off again. No sooner had he lain there awhile than the same thing happened a third time: the Fox jumped up, shouted “Yes!” and darted off to the tub, where he now ate everything that was left. When he came back and the Bear asked him about the latest baby’s name, he replied:

“Licked-the-Bottom!”

They lay down for another good while. Then it was time to go check on the butter, but it was all gone. The Bear blamed the Fox, and the Fox blamed the Bear; each claimed the other had slipped off to the tub while he had been sleeping.

“Well, well,” said the Fox, “we’ll soon find out who stole the butter. Let’s lie down on that sunny slope: whoever’s rear end is the fattest when we wake up—that’s who’s the thief.”

The Bear was more than willing to try, knowing he hadn’t even tasted the butter, and so he settled down confidently in the sun. Meanwhile, the Fox crept off to the tub to find one last bit of butter left in a crack. He fetched it and smeared it on the Bear’s hind end before lying down as if nothing had happened. When they both woke up, the sun had melted the butter, and so it was the Bear who ended up looking as though he’d eaten it all.
