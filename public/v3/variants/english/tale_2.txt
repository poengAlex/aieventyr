Gertrude’s Bird

In those days when our Lord and St. Peter walked on Earth, they once came to a woman who was sitting and baking. She was named Gertrude, and she wore a red cap on her head.

Since they had traveled far and were both hungry, our Lord politely asked her for a flatbread to taste. Yes, he could have one, but she took a tiny piece of dough and rolled it out; still, it grew so large that it covered the entire griddle. No, that flatbread was too big—he couldn’t have it. She took an even smaller piece of dough; but when she rolled it out and spread it on the griddle, that flatbread also became too big—he couldn’t have that one either. The third time, she used an even tinier piece of dough—just a little speck—but again the flatbread grew far too large.

“Then I have nothing to give you,” said Gertrude. “You may as well go on your way without a sample, because all the flatbreads turn out too big.”

At this, our Lord grew angry and said: “Because you begrudged me so harshly, you shall be punished. You must become a bird, peck your dry food between bark and wood, and only drink whenever it rains.”

No sooner had he spoken the last word than she turned into Gertrude’s Bird, flying up from the baking board and out through the chimney pipe. To this day, people say you can see her flying around with her red cap, all black over her entire body—soot-stained from the chimney. She constantly pecks at the trees in search of food and cries out at the sign of rain, for she is always thirsty and then awaits her drink.
