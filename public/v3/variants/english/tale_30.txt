The Boy and the Devil

Once upon a time, there was a Boy who walked along a road, cracking nuts. He found one that was worm-eaten, and at that very moment, he met the Devil.

“Is it true,” said the Boy, “what they say—that the Devil can make himself as small as he wishes, and squeeze through the eye of a needle?”

“Yes!” answered the Devil.

“Ah, let me see it. Crawl into this nut!” said the Boy, and the Devil did so.

Once he had crawled in through the wormhole, the Boy stuck in a small peg. “Now I’ve got you,” he said, and put the nut in his pocket.

After walking for a while, he came to a blacksmith’s workshop. He went in and asked the Blacksmith if he would break open the nut for him.

“Yes, that should be easy enough,” replied the Blacksmith. He took his smallest hammer, set the nut on the anvil, and struck it; but it wouldn’t crack. Then he took a slightly larger hammer, but it still wasn’t heavy enough. So he took an even bigger one, yet that didn’t work either. The Blacksmith became angry and grabbed his big sledgehammer. “I’ll certainly break you,” he said, and struck with all his might. The nut then cracked with such force that half the workshop roof flew off, and it made such a racket, you would have thought the building was about to come crashing down.

“I suppose the Devil was in that nut!” said the Blacksmith.

“Yes, indeed he was,” said the Boy.
