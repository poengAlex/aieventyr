Once upon a time, there was a woman who went out to hire a shepherd. Along the way, she met a bear. "Where are you going?" asked the bear. "Oh, I'm going out to hire a shepherd," the woman replied. "Won't you consider me as your shepherd?" asked the bear. "Well, if you can lure them, then—" said the woman. "Hö—il," said the bear. "No, I won't have you," said the woman upon hearing that, and went on her way.

After walking a bit further, she met a wolf. "Where are you going?" said the wolf. "I'm off to hire a shepherd," the woman answered. "Won't you have me as your shepherd?" asked the wolf. "Yes, if you can lure them, then—" said the woman. "Uh—uh!" said the wolf. "No, I don't want you," said the woman.

Walking a little more, she met a fox. "Where are you going?" asked the fox. "Oh, I need to hire a shepherd," said the woman. "Wouldn't you take me as your shepherd?" asked the fox. "Yes, if only you can lure them, then—" said the woman. "Dil—dal—holom!" said the fox, in a clear and fine voice. "Yes, I'll have you as my shepherd," said the woman, and so she set the fox to look after her livestock.

On the first day, the fox ate all of the woman's goats; on the second day, it finished off all her sheep, and on the third day, it devoured all her cows. When he returned home in the evening, the woman asked where he had left all her livestock. "The skull is in the brook, and the body is in the forest," said the fox. During that time, the woman was churning butter, but she decided she needed to check on the livestock, leaving the fox alone. While she was gone, the fox slipped into the churn and ate the cream. When the woman returned and saw what had happened, she became so angry that she took the little bit of cream that was left and threw it at the fox, splattering the end of its tail; that's why the fox has a white tip on its tail.
