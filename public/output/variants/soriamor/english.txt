Once upon a time, there was a couple who had a son named Halvor. From the time he was a little boy, he never wanted to do anything and just sat by the hearth playing with ashes. His parents sent him away to learn many trades, but Halvor never stayed anywhere for long. Whenever he was gone for a few days, he would run away from his lessons, return home, and sit by the hearth again, digging through the ashes. 

One day, a ship's captain visited and asked Halvor if he wanted to join him on a voyage to foreign lands. Halvor was eager to go, and they set sail. After some time, a storm hit, and when it finally cleared, they found themselves on an unfamiliar coast. They didn't recognize the place, so they stayed put. Halvor asked the captain if he could go ashore to look around, preferring a walk to sleeping on board. 

"Do you think you can face people in the ragged clothes you have?" the captain asked. Halvor insisted, and eventually got permission to go ashore, but had to return when the wind picked up. 

As Halvor roamed the beautiful land, fields spread wide around him, but he saw no people. When the wind began to blow, he felt he hadn't seen enough yet and walked further, hoping to find someone. Eventually, he reached a wide road and, despite his hunger, followed it until he saw a bright castle shining through every window in the distance. As he approached it, he grew increasingly afraid. 

Within the castle, it was warm and inviting. Halvor entered the kitchen, which was more splendid than any he had encountered, with hearths of gold and silver, yet devoid of people. After waiting a while, he tried opening a door and found a princess spinning on a wheel inside. "Oh no!" she exclaimed, "How can a Christian soul dare come here? It's best you leave, lest the three-headed troll devours you." 

"I don't care if it has four heads," Halvor replied, "I'd still like to meet it. And I'm not leaving; I haven't done anything wrong, but you must give me food, for I'm terribly hungry."

After filling his belly, the princess asked him to see if he could wield the sword hanging on the wall. He couldn't even lift it. "Then take a sip from the bottle that hangs beside it," she instructed him, "for that's what the troll does before wielding the sword." 

Halvor took a sip, and suddenly, swinging the sword felt like nothing. So, he waited for the troll to come. When the troll finally arrived, it shouted, "Fie! I smell Christian man's blood," as it stuck its heads through the door. "You'll feel it soon," answered Halvor, and cut off all its heads.

The princess rejoiced at being saved. She danced and sang but soon thought of her sisters, saying, "I wish my sisters were saved too!" When Halvor inquired about them, she explained that one was held by a troll six miles away, and the other nine miles further still. 

"But first, we must clean this mess," the princess said. Halvor was strong, so he quickly cleared away the remains and tidied up. 

He lived well there, and the next morning, eager to set off, he departed at dawn. He walked and ran tirelessly until reaching another castle at twilight. This second castle was grander than the first, but again, there were no people. 

Halvor entered the kitchen and moved further in. Here, another princess warned him of the six-headed troll and advised him to leave to avoid being devoured. Halvor remained undeterred. After eating, he again tried to wield the troll's sword, succeeding only after sipping from the enchanted bottle. 

When the troll came hurling menacingly into the castle, Halvor quickly decapitated it. The princess, elated at her deliverance, thought of her sisters and hoped for their safety. Halvor offered to help and departed the following morning, after tidying up. 

His journey brought him to the third castle, more magnificent than the others. By now, he barely felt any fear. Inside, the third princess, beauty unmatched, repeated the warning: this castle housed a nine-headed troll. 

Halvor declared that even with eighteen or more heads, he would not leave. Again, he drank from the bottle and was able to beat the troll. The last head was the toughest challenge he had faced.

Reunited, the princesses met at one castle and were grateful to Halvor. He could choose whichever sister he liked best, and all of them enjoyed his company. Yet, Halvor grew quiet and pensive, yearning for his parents. The princesses assured him not to worry, promising he could travel safely if he followed their instructions. 

They dressed him finely, as a prince, and gave him a ring that allowed his return journey with a simple wish. However, they warned him never to lose the ring or mention their names or he would never see them again. 

"I wish to be home, and home to be here," Halvor said, and instantly, he stood outside his parents' cabin. It was dusk, and the sight of a richly dressed noble astonished them. When Halvor asked if he could stay the night, they declined, saying they had nothing suitable for someone of his stature. Instead, they suggested seeking lodging at a grand farm nearby, but Halvor insisted on staying. 

He settled by the hearth, digging into the ashes as he used to. In their conversation, Halvor asked if they ever had a child. They replied about a son, Halvor, who was lazy and always played in the ashes. 

"Could it not be me?" Halvor asked. His mother dismissed the idea; their son used to be covered in rags. Then, noting his familiarity, she recognized him, and the parents rejoiced. He shared tales of his adventures, and his mother, eager to show him off, encouraged him to visit the farm's proud girls. 

They doubted Halvor had changed, until he arrived, stunning them with his transformation. Boasting of the princesses he saved, especially the one he loved, Halvor accidentally wished he'd see them again. 

Instantly, the princesses appeared, reminding him of their warning. They received great attention during the visit but chose not to remain long. Accepting Halvor's accompaniment, they journeyed towards the grand castle located by a serene lake.

There, needing rest, the youngest princess offered to stroke his hair, leading Halvor to sleep. When he awoke, the princesses had vanished, using his wish-granting ring. Distraught, Halvor mourned, bidding farewell to his parents, determined to find the princesses again. 

With three hundred dollars left, he set off on the road, acquiring a horse in hopes of reaching them fast. After riding through an endless forest, he reached a poor cottage inhabited by an aged couple. They had not seen Christian folk for hundreds of years but offered to ask the moon for guidance. 

When the moon rose, she couldn't help, but the west wind, arriving shortly after, knew the way to Soria Moria Castle, explaining he'd be drying clothes for a wedding there. Halvor traded his horse to an old lady for boots that allowed him to step equal to fifteen leagues in a stride and followed the wind's rush to the castle.

At long last, Halvor arrived at the castle, where the spectacle of wedding preparations filled the grounds. Disheveled and torn as he was, Halvor waited until the final day. 

When they drank to the bride's health, he dropped the princess’s ring into the cup, signaling her. She instantly recognized her rescuer and declared that only the man who saved them could deserve her, not the groom in place. 

Without delay, Halvor shed his tattered clothes, revealing his noble attire, and claimed his rightful place as the bridegroom, finally uniting with the youngest princess, celebrating their union with joy and merriment.
