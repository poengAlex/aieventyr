Peter Christen Asbjørnsen was born on January 15, 1812, in Kristiania, where his father worked as a glassmaker. He became a student in 1833 and, after spending some years as a private tutor, turned his focus to medicine and natural history, particularly zoology. He soon emerged as a writer on zoological topics and discovered, among other things, an intriguing new starfish, which, along with other findings, shed light on ancient geological conditions. Later, he shifted his attention to practical fields. After studying forestry in Germany from 1856 to 1858, he was appointed as the forest master in the provinces around Trondheim. Some years later, after another scholarship-funded journey, he became the head of the state’s peat survey. He maintained this position until 1876, when he retired. During this period, he authored numerous writings on forestry, peat industry, and other national economic topics, including a cookbook that sparked a prolonged newspaper feud, the so-called "porridge war."

What gives Asbjørnsen his lasting importance to our national cultural life, however, is his work in the service of folklore. Just a year after he became a student, he started to record fairy tales and legends, and in 1842, alongside Jørgen Moe, he published the first volume of "Norwegian Folk Tales, Collected and Told by P. Chr. Asbjørnsen and Jørgen Moe." This collection, of which the seventh edition is presented here, was followed by "Norwegian Folk Tales Told by P. Chr. Asbjørnsen. New Collection. With Contributions from Jørgen Moe's Travels and Notes," in 1871 and 1876. Asbjørnsen also gathered folk legends, publishing them framed in vibrant, fragrant portrayals of the land and its people ("Norwegian Hulder Tales and Folk Legends," 1845—1848). Asbjørnsen passed away in Kristiania on January 6, 1885.
