Once upon a time, far, far away in a great forest, there lived a poor couple. The wife gave birth to a child, a lovely daughter; but being poor, they didn't know how to have the child baptized. So, one day, the husband had to go out to find godparents who could sponsor the baptism themselves; he spent the whole day asking this person and that, but everyone said they would like to be a godparent, although none felt they could afford to offer anything. As he headed home that evening, he met a beautiful lady dressed in splendid clothes, who appeared so kind and gracious. She offered to arrange the baptism but wanted to keep the child afterward. The man replied that he would first have to ask his wife what she thought; but when he told her, the wife flatly refused the offer.

The next day, the man went out again, but still no one was willing to be a godparent unless they were offered gifts. Despite his pleas, nothing changed. As he headed home again in the evening, he met the lovely lady once more, who looked just as kind, and she made the same offer again. He told his wife what had happened, and she said that if he couldn't find godparents for the child the next day either, they should let the lady take the baby since she seemed so good-natured. On the third day, the man found no godparents, and when he met the lady in the evening, he promised to let her take the child as long as she would ensure it was baptized and brought up in Christianity.

The next morning, the lady arrived at the man's home with two men, took the child, and traveled to the church, where the baby was baptized. She then took the child home with her, where the little girl lived for many years. Her foster mother was always kind and good to her. When the girl became old enough to understand, her foster mother prepared to travel. "You can go into every room you wish," she told the girl, "just not into the three chambers I am now showing you." And then she left.

However, the girl couldn't resist opening the door to one of the chambers slightly, and whoosh! A star flew out. When the foster mother returned, she was angry with her foster daughter and threatened to send her away, but the child cried and pleaded, and eventually, she was allowed to stay.

After some time, the foster mother was to travel again, and she again forbade the girl from entering the two chambers she had not visited. The girl promised she would be careful, but after wandering alone and wondering for a while what might be inside the second chamber, she couldn't stop herself from peeking a little inside, and whoosh! The moon flew out. When the foster mother came back and saw that the moon was gone, she became very sad and told the girl that now she couldn’t keep her any longer, she had to leave.

But the girl cried so heartrendingly and pleaded so sweetly, and she was allowed to stay once again. When the foster mother prepared for another trip, she earnestly warned the girl, who was now nearly grown, not to enter or even look into the third chamber. But when the foster mother had been away for a while, and the girl had been alone and bored for a long time, she thought, "Oh, how fun it would be to see inside the third chamber!” At first, she resisted the urge out of respect for her foster mother, but when the thought came to her again, she couldn't hold back; she felt she simply must look inside the chamber. She peeked a little through the door, and whoosh! The sun flew out. When the foster mother returned and saw that the sun had escaped, she became deeply saddened and said that now the girl absolutely could not stay with her anymore. The foster daughter cried and pleaded more beautifully than ever before, but it was no use.

"No, now I must punish you," the foster mother said. "But you have a choice: either you will become the most beautiful woman in the world and not be able to speak, or become the ugliest and be able to talk. But you must leave me.” The girl said, "Then I would rather be beautiful." And so she became, but from that time on, she was mute.

After leaving her foster mother, she wandered through a vast, vast forest, but no matter how far she walked, it seemed never-ending. As evening approached, she climbed high into a tree standing next to a well and settled there to sleep for the night. Nearby lay a castle, and from it, early in the morning, came a girl to fetch water from the well for the prince's tea. The maid saw the beautiful face reflected in the well and thought it was her own; so she threw down her water jug, ran back, and exclaimed, "If I’m so beautiful, I’m too good to be carrying water." Another went for water, but it was the same with her; she, too, returned saying she was too beautiful and too good to fetch water for the prince. Then the prince himself went, curious to see what was happening. When he came to the well, he too saw the reflection and immediately looked up; there, he saw the beautiful maiden sitting high in the tree. He coaxed her down and took her home, insisting on making her his queen, because she was so lovely. But his mother, who was still alive, opposed the idea. "She cannot speak," she remarked, "and she might be a witch." But the prince was so determined that he finally got his wish.

After they had lived together for a while, she became pregnant, and as her time to give birth approached, the prince set a strong guard around her. But during the birth, everyone fell asleep, and as she gave birth, her foster mother came, cut the child's little finger, and smeared the queen's mouth and fingers with the blood, saying to her, "Now you shall be as sorrowful as I was when you let the star escape;" and then she vanished with the baby. When those set to guard the room awoke, they believed the queen had eaten her own child, and the prince's mother insisted she be burned, but the prince loved her dearly, and he managed to plead for mercy, barely saving her.

The second time the young queen was pregnant, they placed an even stronger guard than before. But it happened just the same as previously, except the foster mother said, "Now you shall be as sorrowful as I was when you let the moon escape." The queen wept and pleaded—for when the foster mother was present, she could speak—but it was no use. Now the old queen demanded her to be burned, but once again, the prince managed to save her.

When the queen was to give birth a third time, a triple guard was placed around her, but the same events unfolded: the foster mother arrived while the guards slept, took the child, cut its little finger, and smeared the queen’s mouth with blood; then she said that the queen would be as sorrowful as she herself had been when she let the sun escape. This time, there was no saving her: she was to be burned.

However, just as they were leading her to the pyre, her foster mother appeared with all three children; she held two by the hand and carried the third in her arms. She approached the young queen and said: "Here are your children; now you shall have them back. I am the Virgin Mary, and just as sorrowful as you have been, I was when you let the sun, the moon, and the star escape. Now you have endured your punishment, and from now on you will again be able to speak." 

Everyone can easily imagine how overjoyed the queen and the prince were, though no words can truly express it. From that time on, they were always happy, and the prince's mother came to love the young queen too.
