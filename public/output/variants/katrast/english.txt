Once upon a time, there was a King who had become a widower. From his Queen, he had a daughter who was so kind and beautiful that no one could be kinder or more lovely. The King mourned the loss of the Queen, whom he had loved dearly, for a long time, but eventually, he grew tired of living alone and married a widowed Queen, who also had a daughter. However, her daughter was as ugly and wicked as the King's daughter was kind and beautiful. The stepmother and her daughter were envious of the King's daughter because of her beauty; yet, as long as the King was home, they didn’t dare harm her, for he loved her dearly.

Time passed, and the King went to war with another King. As soon as he was gone, the Queen made the stepdaughter starve and beat her, chasing her into every corner. Eventually, the Queen felt that everything was too good for her and set her to tend the animals. So, she went with the livestock, tending them in the forest and on the mountain. She got little to no food; she became pale and thin, and she almost always wept and was sorrowful. In the herd, there was a large blue bull that stayed clean and glossy, often approaching the King's daughter to let her pet him.

One day, as she sat grieving and weeping again, the bull came over and asked why she was so sorrowful. She said nothing and only cried. "I know," said the bull, "though you don't say it; you cry because the Queen is cruel to you and because she wishes to starve you. But you need not worry about food; in my left ear, there is a cloth, and when you spread it out, you can get as many dishes as you wish." She did as told, spread the cloth on the grass, and it laid out the most delicious dishes anyone could imagine, with wine, mead, and sweet cake. Soon, she regained her health and became so rosy, plump, and fair that the Queen and her withered daughter grew both blue and pale with jealousy.

The Queen couldn’t understand how the stepdaughter could look so well on such poor fare, so she told one of her maids to follow her into the forest and see how it happened, for she suspected the servants must be giving her food. The maid followed into the forest and saw the stepdaughter take the cloth from the blue bull’s ear, spread it out, and feast on the finest dishes it laid out. The maid returned and reported everything to the Queen.

The King returned home, having defeated the other King in battle, and there was great joy throughout the castle, and none happier than the King’s daughter. But then, the Queen feigned illness and bribed the doctor to say that she could only recover by eating the meat of the blue bull. Both the King’s daughter and the servants pleaded with the doctor for another remedy, as everyone loved the bull, saying there was no other like it in the realm, but no, it was decided the bull should be slaughtered.

Hearing of this, the King's daughter was distressed and went down to the barn to see the bull. It looked so mournful with its head hung low that she began to weep again for its sake. "Why do you weep?" asked the bull. She replied that the King had returned, and the Queen was feigning illness, having persuaded the doctor she couldn’t recover unless she ate the meat of the blue bull, and now it was to be slaughtered. "If they take my life, they will surely soon take yours as well," said the bull. "If you feel as I do, let's run away this very night." 

Though reluctant to leave her father, staying in the same house as the Queen seemed even worse, so she promised to come. That evening, when everyone had retired, the King’s daughter snuck down to the barn. The bull then took her on its back and raced away as fast as it could.

When the people arose next morning to slaughter the bull, it was gone, and when the King searched for his daughter, she was missing too. He sent out messengers in every direction to search for them and proclaimed it from the church hill, but no one had seen anything.

Meanwhile, the bull carried the King's daughter through many lands, finally reaching a great copper forest; trees, branches, leaves, and flowers there were all of copper. Before entering the forest, the bull warned the King's daughter: "When we enter, be very cautious you don't touch even a single leaf, for all will be lost for both you and me, for a troll with three heads owns it." She promised to be cautious and not touch anything. She bent and moved aside branches with care but, so dense was the forest, she inevitably plucked a leaf into her hand. "Oh no, what have you done?" cried the bull. "Now we'll have to fight for our lives. Keep the leaf safe."

Shortly after, they reached the end of the forest, and there came a troll charging toward them with three heads. "Who dares touch my forest?" roared the troll. "It is as much mine as yours," retorted the bull. "We shall fight for it!" screamed the troll. "Very well," said the bull, and they clashed fiercely. The bull used all its might to gore and tramp on the troll, but the troll struck back hard, and it took the whole day for the bull to defeat it, by which time it was covered in wounds and so weak it could scarcely move.

They had to rest a day, and the bull instructed the King's daughter to take the troll's healing ointment from its belt and apply it to him. She did, and the next day, he regained some strength, and they continued their journey.

After many more days, they reached a great silver forest where everything—trees, branches, leaves, and flowers—was made of silver. The bull warned again, "Here, be doubly cautious, for a troll with six heads owns it, and I fear defeating it." She promised and tried hard to avoid contact, but the forest became thicker and more impenetrable, and unable to avoid it, she broke off a leaf. "Oh no, what have you done?" the bull lamented. "Now we must fight a six-headed troll." Yet, she kept the leaf safe.

Soon enough, the troll with six heads appeared. "Who dares touch my forest?" it bellowed. "It is as much mine as yours," the bull declared. "We shall fight for it!" screamed the troll. "Very well," agreed the bull, launching at the troll. A ferocious battle ensued, where the bull gouged the troll's eyes and rent its body with its horns, yet the troll, with its mighty blows, fought resiliently. It took three entire days of fierce combat before the bull triumphed, and by then, it was so heavily wounded and spent that it could scarcely move. Once more, the King's daughter used the healing ointment from the troll's belt, reviving the bull, though it needed a week's rest before travel resumed.

Finally, they reached a grand golden forest, resplendent with gold dripping from every branch, among trees, flowers, and leaves all of gold. Again, the bull cautioned, "Take the utmost care you do not disturb even a leaf here, for a nine-headed troll owns it, and it will be impossible to defeat." Despite her promise, the forest was denser than ever and grew ever more constricted as they moved on. She steered her way, but couldn’t avoid catching a golden apple as the branches hit her eyes. Tearful and afraid, she wanted to throw it away, but the bull advised her to keep and protect the apple as best she could, encouraging her not to lose hope for the coming battle.

Sure enough, the monstrous nine-headed troll appeared, terrifying in its form. "Who dares enter my forest?" it thundered. "It is as much mine as yours," the bull boldly stated. "We shall fight for it!" shrieked the troll. "Very well," said the bull, setting upon the troll. The fight was so dire and dreadful on the eyes, and the King's daughter nearly fainted from fear. The bull repeatedly destroyed the troll's heads and gored its body till innards spilled, yet each time a head was taken; the remaining ones revived the fallen. After a whole grueling week, the bull finally overcame the troll, but it fell into an exhausted, near-dead state, unable even to speak to instruct the King's daughter. Nevertheless, she knew to retrieve the healing ointment from the troll's belt and applied it, reviving the bull. But they lay for three whole weeks before the bull was able to carry on.

Then they traveled onwards, over growing forest-clad heights until reaching a mountain. "Do you see anything?" queried the bull. "No, all I see is the sky and those wild hills," she replied. Climbing higher, open terrain allowed her to see farther. "And now?" asked the bull. "Yes, far away, I see a small castle," she said. "It is not so small as you think," replied the bull. Eventually, they reached a great mound with a sheer rock face. "And now?" inquired the bull. "Now the castle is near, and it's much, much larger," she responded. "That is where you must go," said the bull, "Below the castle is a pigsty where you shall reside. There, you will find a wooden cloak you must wear, then go to the castle and say your name is Kari Wood-Cloak, seeking service. Now take your little knife and cut off my head, skin me, roll up the hide, and place it under this rock ledge with the copper leaf, silver leaf, and golden apple inside. Nearby stands a staff—strike the rock wall with it if you ever need me."

At first, she refused, but since it was the only thing he requested in return for all he had done, she couldn’t refuse. With a heavy heart, she hesitated but then ultimately cut through to take off the head and hide, rolling it as instructed, hiding it, and storing the copper leaf, silver leaf, and golden apple within. Afterward, she made her way to the pigsty, all the while weeping and full of despair. Donning the wooden cloak, she proceeded to the royal residence, entering the kitchen to ask for work, introducing herself as Kari Wood-Cloak. The cook acknowledged she could stay and wash up, as the last girl quit recently, though doubting she’d last long in such a role. Nevertheless, she was diligent in her task.

On Sunday, guests were expected at the castle. Kari asked if she could take washwater to the Prince, but she was met with scorn and mockery, wondering why she wanted to go looking so wretched in her cloak. Persisting in her request, at last, she was permitted. As she ascended the steps, her cloak made a clattering sound, catching the Prince's attention, and he came out to see who it was.

"What are you?" asked the Prince. "I'm here to bring washwater," said Kari. "Do you think I'd take water from you?" scoffed the Prince, throwing it over her. She retraced her steps but then asked to go to church, a request met with similar derision, questioning what she wanted there in such a state. Kari claimed the preacher was so good she had to hear him, and persistence won her permission.

She proceeded to the rock with the staff in hand, where the man reappeared with garments to grace her in attire radiant as the copper forest, accompanied by a horse and saddle. Arriving at the church, she drew in curious gazes, none more captivated than the Prince, who could not take his eyes off her. Exiting, the Prince hurriedly caught the door to grab one of her gloves. Once mounted, he inquired where she hailed from. "I’m from Washland," she replied, invoking a charm for dusk ahead and behind, leaving her route unseen to the Prince.

His infatuation with the unknown maiden grew, driving him to futile searches for the fabled land she claimed. The next Sunday sent another opportunity to curry favor, delivering a towel to the Prince, again seeking permission despite ridicule for her persistence. Clamoring once more, she incurred the Prince's wrath, sent away with towel thrown after her.

Attending church again, Kari donned a dress beautifully detailed with silver before her eyes, akin to the silver woods, mounted a horse adorned in silver reins and coverings. The congregation buzzed with intrigue; the Prince lost all composure, engrossed with the mysterious lady. Post-service, the Prince raced out, asking her origin. "From Towel-land," Kari replied, dropping her riding whip which the Prince retrieved but saw nothing of her departure, struck by her concealment charm once more.

With no trace found of the mysterious maiden, word spread of a quest to fit a princess slipper, promising marriage to the one who fit her slipper. From far and wide came ladies eager to try, but none matched until Kari's turn. As she mounted the steps, clattering resounded, and ridiculed, she endured the charade, until slipping the golden shoe onto her foot, casting away her wooden cloak, revealing herself in radiant attire, with the matching shoe adorning the opposite foot. The Prince recognized her immediately, overjoyed, embracing her, and upon learning her royal lineage, his happiness grew. Thus commenced their wedding celebration.

Snip, snap, snout, now the tale is out.
