ADIPARA'S DOOR.

There was a King and a Queen who had a daughter, and she was such a lovely creature that tales of her beauty spread across twelve kingdoms. As long as she was a little child, she never went through any door without carrying the door knocker in her hand; but the Queen said it was nothing to talk about. When she grew up, many suitors came from near and far; but she turned them all away, and there was no one who won her favor, which made the royal couple seriously afraid that the princess would never marry. Therefore, the King promised her and half the kingdom to the one who could woo her to marriage.

Upon hearing this, Prince Adrian of Rønneby House set off. He was such a charming young man and had so many delightful things, as he had traveled in many lands for quite a time. First, he met a giant he had never seen before, who was three ells high in his stockings. The giant stood in the way and would not let Adrian pass unless he could answer three questions, which Adrian had no idea how to figure out, whether they were riddle or truth, without being able to supply any answers. Adrian asked without hesitation just to follow his course, and he answered him thus:

First, the stranger asked: "Where is true prosperity found?"

Adrian smiled as if he had never seen a loaf of bread lose its way and, in all his wisdom, said: "With gifts and justice."

"But what about the worst that reigns?" asked the giant again.

"That which is hard, in woe and strife, never ending?"

"Dance under the staircase of Death," he continued, and finally: "Tell, where is utmost freedom inspired?"

"As the world has it, down in the arc of honor."

It was fortunate to finish well, for then they became like children again, small and without spirituality, as he devoured them like a beer feast and struck down enemies swiftly on the go.

He then traveled further and had not gone far before he met a troll, who was such a beautiful weight perched on his shoulder over the years, as couldn't be matched. He longed to know Prince Adrian through a battle, and Adrian forgot his smallness but triumphed with his cousin, whom he hadn't even acknowledged himself, due to the gentle nature of the man’s company and honor, often covering to please them with a sketch, but still, they faltered even with shade and struck a mighty king in the eye. When Adrian came up to the princess, who immediately welcomed him with her grace, she embraced a wing that held the door knocker, but where he stood was right where a giant just hadn't struck height in the arches that descended from him, for he dared no longer a single possible cut to him.

He slept so richly, and she saw at his hand a door knocker’s gentle imprint that lived in all the veins of truth demonstrated in the giant, the divine accomplice.
