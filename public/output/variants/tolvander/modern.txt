Once upon a time, there was a queen who went for a drive in the winter. It had just snowed, and the landscape was covered in fresh, white snow. On her way, she suddenly got a nosebleed and had to stop the car. Standing beside the road, watching the red blood drip onto the white snow, she thought about how she had twelve sons but no daughter. She wished she had a daughter with skin as white as the snow and lips as red as the blood, even if it meant she would lose her sons. 

Just as she had this thought, a mysterious old woman appeared. "A daughter you shall have," the woman said, "as white as snow and as red as blood. But your sons will become mine. However, you can keep them until the child is baptized." 

When the time came, the Queen gave birth to a daughter, fair as snow and red as blood, just as the old woman had promised. They named her Snow White and Rose Red. The royal castle was filled with joy, but the queen was plagued by her promise to the strange lady. She commissioned a silversmith to make twelve silver spoons and a thirteenth for her daughter.

Right after the baptism, the princes transformed into twelve wild ducks and flew away, leaving the Queen in sorrow. Snow White and Rose Red grew up to be a beautiful young lady but often seemed sad and melancholy. No one knew why.

One evening, when the queen was especially downcast, she asked her daughter why she seemed so sorrowful. Snow White and Rose Red replied, "I feel lonely without siblings. All others have siblings, but I am alone." 

The Queen confessed, "You had siblings. I had twelve sons, your brothers, but I lost them to gain you," and she recounted the entire story.

With this revelation, the princess could find no rest. Despite her mother’s tears and pleas, she felt responsible and insisted on searching for her lost brothers. Finally, she left the palace.

She wandered far and wide, traversing vast forests for what seemed like an eternity. One night, exhausted, she fell asleep on a mossy mound and dreamt of a small cabin in the forest where her brothers lived.

When she awoke, she saw a winding path leading deeper into the woods, like the one in her dream. Following it, she eventually arrived at a small wooden cabin. Inside were twelve beds, chairs, spoons, and pairs of everything else.

She tidied up, cooked a meal, and then, exhausted, hid under the youngest brother's bed. She hadn’t been there long when a sound of flapping and whirring filled the air, and twelve wild ducks flew in through the door, transforming back into princes.

"Who could have made it so warm and cozy here?" they wondered, grateful for the meal. Each prince reached for his spoon, but an extra lay on the table. "This is our sister's spoon," they declared. "She must be nearby." 

Though the oldest prince proposed they harm her for their plight, the youngest argued against it, believing their mother was to blame. As they searched high and low, they found her under the youngest brother's bed.

Initially, the eldest prince wanted to harm her, but she pleaded, "Spare me. I've searched for you for so long. If I could save you, I would gladly risk my life." 

The princes replied, "If you want to save us, gather bog cotton, spin, weave, and sew it into shirts, scarves, and caps. You must not speak, laugh, or cry during this time; then we'll be free."

Snow White and Rose Red asked where she could find so much bog cotton. The princes took her to a vast bog, shimmering with the white cotton under the sun. She began gathering it tirelessly, bringing it back each night to spin and weave.

As time passed, she continued her task and took care of the princes, who lived as humans at night but turned into wild ducks during the day.

One day, while she was on the moor gathering cotton, the young king of the realm happened upon her. He was taken by her beauty and, despite receiving no responses to his questions, decided to take her to the castle to marry her.

The king’s men placed her on his horse, reluctantly allowing her to bring her bags of work. The king, charmed by her beauty and kindness, held a grand wedding, and Snow White and Rose Red became queen.

Despite the joy of her new life, she never forgot her task. She continued working on the shirts, determined to save her brothers.

Before the year was over, Snow White and Rose Red bore the king a son. The king's jealous stepmother saw her opportunity to destroy the new queen. Secretly, she took the baby to a snake pit, injuring the queen’s finger to smear blood around her lips as evidence of cannibalism.

The stepmother told the king that the queen had devoured their child. Heartbroken but unable to believe it, the king begged for patience, convinced it was a misunderstanding.

Yet the cycle repeated with their second son and then a daughter. Each time, the wicked stepmother stole the child, smeared blood around the queen’s mouth, and accused her of the atrocity.

At last, when the stepmother’s lies seemed irrefutable, the king, in great sorrow, sentenced Snow White and Rose Red to be burned at the stake.

As the fire was prepared, she indicated to the servants to lay the clothes of her brothers around the pyre. No sooner had she done so than the sky filled with flapping and twelve wild ducks flew down. Each grabbed a piece of the clothing in its beak, transforming back into princes, except the youngest, who still bore one wing for an arm.

Seeing this, the king halted the execution. The princes explained how each time their sister had a child, the stepmother had taken it. They led the king to the snake pit, where the royal children played unharmed.

Overcome with relief and anger, the king decreed a punishment for the stepmother, who suffered the fate she intended for the queen.

The royal family was joyfully reunited, including the twelve brothers. Snow White and Rose Red returned to her parents, who rejoiced in their reunion. And they all lived happily ever after, thankful for the family they had regained.
