**How Bamsefar Became a Suitor**

Once upon a time, there was a bear named Bamsefar who lived in an animal park. He grew fond of rice pudding, radishes, celery, milk, and so on. He became so tame that he was allowed to wander around the village. But one day, after he had stolen a pig, the villagers were no longer kind to him, and he was chained up.

Now, it happened that Bamsefar wanted to get married. He had set his eyes on a lovely white bear. She was a bit particular, so Bamsefar thought he should present himself properly in town and stand out among the other suitors. He decided to speak with someone knowledgeable about courtship.

"It’s not easy to advise," said the advisor. "Firstly, you must be well-dressed, choose your words carefully, keep your thoughts in order, and if you make a coarse mistake, make up for it with joviality. Ensure that her favorite companions don’t get bored, bring sunshine when it rains, and if you see she has a ring without a pearl, it signals she may have broken more than a bond."

Bamsefar found this advice impressive but not entirely clear.

He then approached another person known for his cheerfulness.

"You should start by laying a foundation for your speech," this one said. "And when you expect rain and sunshine on the same day, make sure the clouds clear up. Then you'll understand how a sunbeam can create good moods and invite people to dance, at least at a fair."

Bamsefar found this challenging, so he thought he must adopt a strong yet gentle role to get his way, and indeed, there was some success.

There were many talks beforehand. Finally came the big day: they walked hand in hand through the town, and at the first dance, there was such a joy that even the small children noticed. After that, a silence akin to a song filled the air, with everyone moving under the lantern light.

As the travel guide went by, the boys strove around like a flock of ravens. This led to merriment and a surprising tale taking shape. Could they believe, Bamsefar faced a great feat ahead.

But on this occasion, Bamsefar planned his wedding as something he knew well, and it ended with sweetness in laughter. Yet, the lesson here is about a wedding not detailed in thickets and hills, for life flows around the farms and the half-filled bales of a farmer’s cart.

The moral is simply that two bears can live together, even with obstacles in their path.

Snip, snap, snout; and so the story is out.
