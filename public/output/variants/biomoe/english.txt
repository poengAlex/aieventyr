Jørgen Moe was the son of a farmer from the farm Mo in Hole in Ringerike, where he was born on April 22, 1813. After becoming a student in 1830, he was for a time deeply engrossed in aesthetics and literature, but after some indecision, he chose theology as his field of study and completed his theological degree in 1839. Even during his studies, he had to support himself by giving lessons; from then on, he taught at various schools and served as a private tutor for a couple of years at Jacob Aall's ironworks in Nes, until he was appointed to the War School in 1845, where he worked for eight years. At the same time, in 1849–1850, he was a university fellow in folk traditions. In 1853, he transitioned to ecclesiastical work, first as a resident chaplain in Krødsherred, later as a parish priest in Drammen, and ultimately in Vestre Aker. From there, in 1875, he was called to be the bishop of Kristiansand, where he died on March 27, 1882.

Moe’s interests led him early towards poetic production. However, his "Poems" were not published until 1849. Two years later came the children's stories "The Well and the Pond," and in 1855, a collection of religious poems. Moe’s significance for Norwegian literature, like that of Asbjørnsen, is mainly owed to the classic rendition of folktales. He began his collections in 1834, and three years later, he partnered with Asbjørnsen to retell and publish both of their notes. For the second edition of the folktales in 1851, Moe wrote an introduction in which he, among other things, provides a characterization of the fairy tales of various cultures, pointing out the character types in the Norwegian ones. He also published the first small collection of Norwegian folk songs and ballads in 1840 and recorded a significant number of old songs, some of which have been published in the collections by Sophus Bugge and Svend Grundtvig.
