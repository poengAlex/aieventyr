In those days when our Lord and Saint Peter walked and wandered the earth, they once came upon a woman who was baking. Her name was Gertrude, and she wore a red cap on her head. Having traveled for a long time, both were hungry, so our Lord politely asked her for a taste of a flatbread. Yes, he would get one, but the woman took a tiny bit of dough and rolled it out; still, it became so large it filled the entire griddle. No, that flatbread was too big; he could not have it. She took an even smaller piece of dough, but when she had baked it and spread it on the griddle, this flatbread too was too big; he could not have that one either. The third time, she took an even smaller piece, a minuscule one; yet this time also, the flatbread turned out too large. "I have nothing to give you," said Gertrude. "You might as well leave without a single taste, for all the flatbreads are too large." Then our Lord became angry and said: "Because you were so begrudging, you shall suffer the punishment of becoming a bird, taking your dry food from between bark and wood, and only getting something to drink when it rains." And scarcely had he spoken the final word, when she turned into the Gertrude bird and flew from the baking table up through the chimney. Even to this day, you can see her flying around with her red cap and entirely black across her body, marked by the soot from the chimney. She constantly pecks and picks at the trees for food and chirps for rain, always thirsty as she awaits a drink.
