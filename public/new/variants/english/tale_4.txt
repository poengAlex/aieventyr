4. The Deflection.

Once upon a time, there was a king who had a daughter who was so tricky and twisty with words that no one could outsmart her. He put out the decree that whoever could do it would win the princess for a wife and half the kingdom. Three brothers who heard this also wanted to go out and try their luck. The two oldest brothers went first, thinking they were the smartest, but they made no progress with her and were punished. Then, the youngest, called Askeladden, set off. As he traveled a bit, he found a wicker loop; he picked it up. A little further, he found a piece of broken shell; he picked that up too. Traveling a bit further, he found a dead magpie, and a little later, a curved goat horn; shortly after, he found another one similar. As he was walking over the fields by the king's farm, where there was manure spread out, he found an old worn-out shoe sole. He took all of these things with him to the king's farm, and then he came to the princess.

"Good day," he said.

"Good day," she replied, looking irritated.

"May I roast my magpie?" he asked.

"I'm afraid she'll burst," the princess replied.

"Oh, no need to worry, I'll wrap her with this wicker loop," the boy said, showing her.

"The fat will drip off her," said the princess.

"I'll catch it with this," said the boy, showing the piece of broken shell.

"You are so shifty with your words," said the princess.

"No, I am not shifty, but this is," he said, picking up one of the goat horns.

"I've never seen the likes!" cried the princess.

"Here you see the likes," said the boy, showing the other horn.

"I think you're worn-out trying to outsmart me, aren't you?" said the princess.

"No, I'm not worn-out, but this is," the boy answered, showing the worn-out shoe sole.

And then, the princess was outsmarted.

"You are now mine," said the boy, and thus, he won her and half the land and kingdom, to boot.
