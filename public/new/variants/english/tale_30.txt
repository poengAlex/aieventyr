The Boy and the Devil.

Once upon a time, there was a boy who was walking along a path cracking nuts; then he found one that was worm-eaten, and just then, he met the Devil. "Is it true," the boy asked, "what they say, that the Devil can make himself as small as he wants, and squeeze through the eye of a needle?" "Yes!" answered the Devil. "Oh, let me see it, and crawl into this nut!" said the boy, and the Devil did. Once he had crawled through the wormhole, the boy quickly plugged it. "Now I've got you," he said, putting the nut in his pocket.

After he had walked a bit, he came to a blacksmith's forge; he went in and asked the blacksmith if he could crack the nut open for him. "Yes, that should be easily done," replied the blacksmith, " and he took his smallest hammer, placed the nut on the anvil, and struck; but the nut would not break. Then he took a slightly larger hammer, but it was still not heavy enough; he then took an even larger one, but it did not do the trick either; and then the blacksmith enraged and took his sledgehammer. "I will crack you open," he said and struck with all his might; and the nut shattered, the roof of the forge was blown half off, and it was as if the whole cottage might come crashing down. "I think the Devil himself was in that nut!" exclaimed the blacksmith. "Yes, indeed he was," said the boy.
