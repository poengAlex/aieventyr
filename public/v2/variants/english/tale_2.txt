The Bird of Gjertrud.

In the days when our Lord and St. Peter walked and wandered on Earth, they once entered a house of a woman who was baking. Her name was Gjertrud, and she had a red cap on her head. Having walked a long time, and both feeling hungry, our Lord kindly asked her for a piece of lefse to taste. Yes, he was to get that, but she took a tiny piece of dough and rolled it out; however it became so large that it filled the entire rack. No, that lefse was too big; he could not have it. She took an even smaller piece of dough; but when she had baked it and spread it on the rack, that lefse was also too large; he could not have it either. The third time, she took an even smaller piece of dough, a tiny bit; but even this time, the lefse was far too big.

"I have nothing to give you", said Gjertrud. "You might as well leave without a bite, for the lefses are too large for all."

Then our Lord grew angry and said, "Because you grudged me so poorly, you shall have the punishment that you shall become a bird, and take your dry food between bark and wood, and not get something to drink more often than when it rains."

And barely had he uttered the last word, she became the bird of Gjertrud, and flew from behind the baking stones up through the chimney; and to this day, one can still see her flying around with her red cap, entirely black all over her body, as the chimney had sooted her. She pecks and pokes constantly at trees for food, and chirps at the rain; for she is always thirsty, and thus, she waits for a drink.
