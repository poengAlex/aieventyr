Bird Dam.

Once upon a time, there was a king who had twelve daughters. He cared for them so deeply that they always had to be with him. However, every midday when the king slept, the princesses went out walking. One day, as the king took his nap and the princesses went out, they vanished and never returned. This caused immense sorrow throughout the land, with the king being the most sorrowful of all. He sent messengers throughout his and foreign lands, announcing their disappearance from every church and ringing all the bells across the country, but the princesses remained missing, and no one knew where they had gone. Therefore, it was realized that they had been taken into the mountains. The news traveled far across towns, villages, and foreign lands, eventually reaching a distant king who had twelve sons. Upon hearing about the twelve missing princesses, the sons asked their father for permission to go in search of them. Reluctant to let them go, fearing he might never see them again, their father finally agreed after much persuasion. He equipped a ship for them and appointed Sir Red as the captain, as he was well-acquainted with sea navigation.

They sailed for a long time, visiting every land they came to, searching and inquiring about the princesses, but to no avail. Now, with only a few days left before they had been at sea for seven years, they faced a fierce storm that made them believe they would never reach land again, compelling everyone to work tirelessly without sleep while the storm persisted. After three days, the storm passed and there was sudden calm. Exhausted from the work and harsh weather, they all fell asleep immediately except for the youngest prince, who could neither rest nor sleep. As he paced back and forth on deck, the ship approached a small island. On it was a little dog barking and yelping at the ship as if it wanted aboard.

The prince called and whistled to the dog from the deck, but the more the dog barked and yelped. He felt pity for the dog, believing it might have come from a ship lost in the storm. Yet, he thought he could do nothing as he doubted his ability to launch the boat alone, and the others were asleep, rendering him reluctant to wake them for the sake of a dog. However, with the weather calm and bright, he decided to go ashore and save the dog. Attempting to launch the boat, he found it easier than expected. He rowed to the island, approaching the dog, but whenever he reached for it, the dog sidestepped each time until he inadvertently entered a magnificent castle.

There, the dog turned into a beautiful princess, and on a bench sat a man so big and ugly that he startled the prince. "Don't be afraid," said the man — yet the prince became even more frightened hearing his voice — "I know what you're here for: there are twelve of you princes, and you're searching for the twelve missing princesses. I know where they are; they are with my master, each sitting on a gold chair, grooming him, for he has twelve heads. You've sailed for seven years, but you'll sail for another seven before you find them. You could stay here," he said, "and marry my daughter, but you must first kill my master; he is a harsh lord to us, whom we are all tired of, and when he is dead, I will become king in his place. But first, try to swing the sword," said the troll.

The prince grabbed an old rusty sword hanging on the wall but could hardly move it. "Take a sip from this bottle," the troll said. Having done so, he managed to move the sword; after another sip, he could lift it, and with another, he swung it as effortlessly as a lightweight stick. "When you board the ship again," said the troll prince, "hide the sword well in your bunk so Sir Red won't see it; he can't wield it, but he will grow spiteful and seek your life. When seven years pass with only three days remaining," he continued, "it'll happen the same as now: a strong storm will come over you, and when it subsides, you'll all become sleepy. That's when you must take the sword, row ashore, and find a castle. No need to fear the guards — wolves, bears, and lions; they will fall at your feet. Inside, you'll find him in a grand chamber, splendidly furnished; but he has twelve heads, and the princesses sit on each gold chair, grooming each head. They do not take pleasure in this task. You must hurry to chop off each head; if he wakes and sees you, he will swallow you alive."

The prince boarded with the sword, memorizing what he learned. The others were still asleep, and he concealed the sword in his bunk, unnoticed by Sir Red or the others. The wind picked up again, prompting him to wake the others, saying they shouldn't sleep as it was a good wind, unaware of his earlier absence.

When the seven years lapsed with three days remaining, circumstances unfolded as the troll predicted. A severe storm ensued for three days, and once it passed, all grew drowsy from the labor and lay down. The youngest prince rowed ashore, approached the castle where guards fell before him, and entered. Inside, just as the troll prince described, the great troll slept, and the twelve princesses sat, each grooming a head. The prince gestured for the princesses to move aside. They motioned towards the troll, gesturing for him to leave, yet he persisted in signaling them to make way, making them realize he intended to rescue them. Quietly, one by one, they moved, and quickly he beheaded the troll king, making blood flow like a great stream. Once the troll was slain, he returned to the ship with the sword, feeling his task was sufficient. Unable to dispose of the body alone, he awoke the others, saying it was shameful to sleep while he had rescued the princesses from the troll.

The others laughed, suggesting he had dreamt of being so heroic, for if anyone saved the princesses, one of them would be more likely. The youngest prince recounted all the events, and upon reaching land, they first witnessed the blood stream, then the castle, the troll, the twelve heads, and the princesses, verifying his truthfulness. They helped throw the heads and body into the sea. Now, all rejoiced, especially the princesses, relieved from tending the troll all day. From all the gold, silver, and treasures present, they took as much as the ship could carry, boarding together — princes and princesses alike.

As they sailed a bit out, the princesses realized in their joy they had forgotten their gold crowns resting in a cabinet, which they dearly wished to retrieve. With no one else willing to go, the youngest prince offered: “I’ve ventured much before, so I can fetch the gold crowns too, provided you lower the sails and wait for my return.” They agreed.

Once he journeyed far enough to be out of sight, Sir Red, who desired to be the most distinguished and win the youngest princess, insisted it was senseless to wait, as they could guess he would never return. He claimed the king had granted him — Sir Red — authority, permitting him to sail at will and declare he rescued the princesses. Any contradiction meant death. The princes, fearing retribution, complied with Sir Red's wishes and set sail.

Meanwhile, the youngest prince rowed ashore, reached the castle, and fetched the cabinet with the gold crowns, hauling and dragging it into the boat. But upon reaching the vantage where he could see the ship, it was gone. Realizing what transpired, he knew chasing after it was pointless and rowed back ashore, understanding his predicament. Though frightened of spending the night alone in the castle, he had no other shelter, resolved to brace his heart, lock every door and port, and lay in a room with a prepared bed. Yet as he lay, once a commotion echoed through the walls and roof as though the whole castle would collapse. Suddenly a pile dropped beside the bed like a load of hay. Silence resumed, and he heard a voice, assuring, “Do not fear, I am Bird Dam, and I will help you forward. On waking tomorrow, retrieve four barrels of rye from the storehouse for my breakfast, or else I can do nothing.”

Awakening, he found a remarkably large bird with a neck feather as thick as a half-grown log. Fetching four barrels of rye for Bird Dam, it devoured them and then instructed the prince to hang the cabinet of gold crowns on one side of its neck, balance with equal weighted gold and silver on the other, and sit securely on its back, gripping tightly onto its neck feather. And off they soared, whizzing through the air, soon passing by the ship. The prince wished to board to retrieve the sword, fearing another spotting it, as the troll warned, but Bird Dam advised otherwise. “Sir Red likely won't see it,” said the bird, “but aboard, he seeks your life, desiring the youngest princess, yet with her, you need not worry; she places a bare sword before her in bed each night.”

Eventually, they reached the troll prince, where the prince received an overly warm reception. The troll prince jubilantly offered hospitality, grateful for the slaying of his master and transition to kingship. Eager to reward the prince, he offered his daughter and half the kingdom, yet the prince’s heart remained steadfast for the youngest princess, compelling him to leave repeatedly. The troll advised patience, reminding him almost seven years remained till reaching home, reiterating Bird Dam’s assurance: “For her, you need not worry; she places a bare sword before her in bed.”

When their ship neared, having endured another storm, the prince boarded while everyone slept, each princess beside their prince, except the youngest, who lay alone with a bare sword before her, Sir Red at the bed’s foot. Capturing the sword unnoticed, he returned ashore, continuing his anxious pacing until merely three weeks remained of seven years. The troll king then bid him prepare for travel since he could not stay longer, granting him the loan of his self-moving iron boat. "Say 'Boat, go forth!'" instructed the troll. A loaded iron club within could be adjusted when the ship came directly ahead, creating a wind to distract them; upon nearing the ship, adjusting the club again would cause such a storm they wouldn’t notice him, and after passing, adjusting a third time would keep them preoccupied, provided he carefully managed it to avoid catastrophe. Once reaching land, he needn't worry about the boat, merely shoving and turning it back, saying, “Boat, go home the way you came!”

Setting out, he received ample gold, silver, splendid items, and clothing stitched by the troll princess over the extended period, becoming wealthier than his brothers. He sat in the boat, uttered “Boat, go forth!” and it went. Spotting the ship ahead, he adjusted the club, creating a tailwind they couldn’t ignore. On reaching the ship’s side, another adjustment caused a fierce storm with waves crashing over the deck, preoccupying them again, and after easing past, a third adjustment ensured their distraction.

Landing long before the ship, he unloaded his possessions, nudged the boat back, and stated, “Boat, go home the way you came!” which it did. Disguising himself as a sailor, he sought refuge in a humble hut with an elderly woman. He told her he was a poor sailor from a wrecked large ship, the sole survivor, seeking shelter for himself and the salvaged items. “By God,” exclaimed the woman, “I can’t provide shelter; you see how it is here; I lack even my own bed, let alone one for others.” “It doesn’t matter,” replied the sailor; if he could find a roof over his head, he’d accept any conditions; shelter was undeniable if content with what she had.

By evening, he settled his belongings inside, and once there, the curious old woman, eager for news to spread, began questioning who he was, where he came from, where he’d been, where he was going, what his cargo was, his purpose, and if he’d heard about the twelve princesses who vanished ages ago, delving into much beyond one could enumerate.

He feigned frailty, claiming a severe headache from the harsh weather, lacking clarity on anything, urging her to allow a few days' rest to recover from labor before revealing all she wished to know, and more. The following day she resumed her interrogations, but his headache from the weather remained debilitating, hindering clear responses, yet occasionally hinting he knew something about the princesses. Immediately she spread her gleanings among the neighboring gossip enthusiasts, prompting one visitor after another seeking fresh updates on the princesses — if he’d seen them, arrival times, trajectory, and other inquiries. He continued his headache complaints, limiting clarity, yet hinted their possible return in about a fortnight or sooner, not guaranteeing survival since last sighting amidst the fierce storm that perhaps swallowed them.

One informant hastened to the royal palace with the sailor’s tale, declaring he’d seen the princesses, expected back in about a fortnight, or maybe in eight days. On hearing this, the king summoned the sailor for firsthand recounting. “I’m not presentable enough,” protested the sailor, “and lack proper attire for such an audience.” Regardless, the king's envoy insisted his presence was mandatory, for none had shared any news of the princesses yet.

At the court, the king inquired about the princesses’ whereabouts. “Yes, I’ve seen them,” the sailor confirmed, “yet can’t be sure they’ve survived, as when last seen, the weather was such that we shipwrecked; but if alive, they might return in about a fortnight, maybe sooner.” The king, overjoyed, almost beside himself, prepared for their anticipated arrival, reaching the shore in full regalia, celebrated nationwide as the ship docked with princesses, princes, and Sir Red, but none happier than the old king having his daughters back.

The eleven eldest princesses radiated joy and liveliness, yet the youngest destined for Sir Red, wept and remained despondent. Troubled, the king questioned her uncharacteristic gloom amidst liberation from the troll and obtaining a husband like Sir Red. Yet, she dared not speak for fear of Sir Red's threat on revealing the reality. One day, while working on bridal attire, a man in a large sailor's coat with a merchant's chest on his back entered, offering treasures of gold and silver for the wedding. Indeed intrigued, they perused the merchandise and scrutinized him, suspecting familiarity with both dealer and goods.

“Surely one with so much splendor,” mused the youngest princess, “has something even grander, befitting us more.” Such might be, acknowledged the vendor, though the others hushed her, reminding of Sir Red's threats. Later, as the princesses peered from a window, the prince returned in a sailor’s coat, carrying the cabinet with gold crowns. Reaching the great hall within the royal premises, he unveiled the cabinet, revealing each princess’s gold crown. The youngest declared, “I believe whoever saved us ought to earn rightful reward, not Sir Red, but the one delivering our gold crowns — he saved us.”

Shedding his sailor’s attire, the prince stood splendidly dressed, surpassing all others in elegance, resulting in Sir Red’s execution by the old king. Finally, true joy embraced the royal court; each took their rightful partner, and the wedding reverberated its grandeur across twelve kingdoms.
